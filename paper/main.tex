\begin{abstract}
Desert microorganisms sustain biogeochemical processes under extreme water and carbon limitation, but genomic evidence and environmental measurements constrain different aspects of their response to change. We developed a causal--ontological digital twin of the Rub' al-Khali, the world's largest continuous sand desert, from 551 primary soil metagenomes collected across five campaigns and 5,438 reference genomes. The twin separates invariant definitions, deduced from OWL axioms and genomic evidence, from causal mechanisms that interventions replace. Deduced carrier memberships set community-specific rate factors, interventions on water, salt and pore conditions leave every deduced quantity unchanged, and the graph identifies interventions that are ambiguous until a version is specified. Genome linkage resolves ambiguous hydrogenase--carbon-fixation overlap in \AmbiguousSamples{} samples and supports \HtwoCBB{} candidate genomes. Matched site--campaign comparisons locate higher candidate shares in subsurface sand. Independent Namib and Sonoran observations test the transfer of the twin's moisture-response mechanisms. Hydration increases measured uptake, but the reference parameterization misses dry Namib activity and the continued increase at high Sonoran moisture. In Sonoran field measurements, environmental calibration reduces held-campaign error from 1.65 to 0.69~nmol~H$_2$~m$^{-2}$~s$^{-1}$, compared with 0.79 for a training-mean baseline. Transfer between three temperate soils also varies by environment. Within the Rub' al-Khali, a fixed 12~mm water addition changes ranking with pulse timing across parameter settings, and communities without mapped CO carriers have zero CO response by deduction. The twin connects primary desert observations, deductive biological constraints and causal intervention calculations, while external tests identify the parameters and measurement correspondences needed for transfer.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Desert ecosystems depend on microorganisms that remain viable and sustain metabolism under severe water limitation. Atmospheric hydrogen and carbon monoxide supply energy, and some microorganisms couple this metabolism to carbon fixation \cite{ji2017atmospheric,leung2020energetic,jordaan2020gobi}. These processes provide an entry point for modelling biological function where vegetation cover and organic inputs are sparse. The Rub' al-Khali, the world's largest continuous sand desert, offers a geographically extensive setting in which to study these microbial contributions \cite{nasaRubAlKhali}.

A digital twin of such an ecosystem must connect observations of biological capacity to predictions about environmental change. Metagenomes establish which genes occur in reconstructed genomes. Water content, pore structure and gas concentrations constrain the physical conditions under which those capacities can be expressed. The connection requires biological definitions as well as causal mechanisms: hydrogen oxidation and carbon fixation detected in a community may belong to different organisms, and an organism carrying both pathways still requires suitable conditions and substrates to use them. Preserving these distinctions is essential when asking how wetting, salinity or disturbance changes ecosystem function.

Metabolic annotation tools, including DRAM and METABOLIC, organize genome evidence into pathways and potential biogeochemical roles \cite{shaffer2020dram,zhou2022metabolic}. Soil process models connect moisture-dependent biological activation with gas transport \cite{bertagni2021moisture}. Digital-twin research is also extending Earth observation and vegetation models to dryland monitoring and restoration. The funded TwinDryLands programme describes planned integration of Earth observation, machine-learning emulation and causal-driver analysis, with completion scheduled for 2030 \cite{twindrylands}. Connecting genome-resolved desert observations to an executable account of biological meaning and intervention consequences presents a complementary methodological problem.

Ontological deduction and causal inference address different parts of this connection. Ontological axioms determine the consequences of biological definitions, including pathway conjunctions, alternative reactions and the meaning of a measured quantity \cite{owl2direct2012}. Causal mechanisms determine how physical states change when specified inputs are manipulated \cite{pearl2009causality}. In a structural causal model, an intervention replaces mechanisms; definitions and other non-causal constraints must hold in every regime \cite{bochman2015pearls}. Separating the two zones has concrete consequences for a digital twin. It determines which quantities can be manipulated, so that water storage can be set but uptake or a pathway label cannot. It makes deduced quantities identical across regimes, which yields zero effects that follow from the evidence without simulation. It also identifies interventions on defined quantities, such as total water potential, or on broad treatments, such as a fixed amount of wetting, that are ambiguous until a version is specified \cite{spirtes2004ambiguous,vanderweele2013multiple}.

Here we construct EQ-BioDT from primary Rub' al-Khali sampling and connect genome-level deduction to conditional soil-process interventions. To our knowledge, this is the first implemented desert microbial digital twin combining primary genome-resolved observations, ontological deduction and causal intervention modelling. We first establish the sampling footprint and the biological inferences supported by the data. We then test the moisture-response mechanisms against independent measurements from the Namib and Sonoran deserts and against contrasting temperate soils. These comparisons distinguish transferable responses from environmental parameters that require calibration, providing an empirical basis for interpreting scenario predictions. Data, ontological definitions, model code and reproducible analyses are available in the public repository.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Primary observations instantiate a geographically explicit desert twin}
The twin represents 551 soil metagenomes collected during five campaigns between March 2023 and October 2025. These observations cover 217 site--campaign visits at 61 locations: 59 of the 60 core sites on the repeated Rub' al-Khali transect and two auxiliary locations. The wider survey, including amplicon data, covers all 60 core sites; no metagenome is available from site 56. The sampled footprint extends from 45.17 to 54.73$^\circ$E and from 19.15 to 20.92$^\circ$N (Fig.~\ref{fig:primary}). Surface sand, subsurface sand and root-adjacent soil contribute 117, 166 and 268 metagenomes, respectively. The spatial and temporal distribution of these specimens defines the observation support of the twin.

Each specimen links to read-based marker measurements and mapping against a catalogue of \ReferenceMAGs{} metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs). Genome annotations support pathway classification, while mapping weights locate the corresponding capacities in the sampled environment. Of the 551 specimens, \MappedSamples{} have usable mapped compositions. The provenance chain therefore connects field location and collection date to molecular observations, deductive pathway support and the community factors that enter the process mechanisms (Fig.~\ref{fig:primary}C). Kinetic and soil-retention parameters enter as separately specified physical quantities.

\subsection{Ontological deduction constrains the biological interpretation of interventions}
A prediction of hydrogen-supported carbon fixation requires evidence that the relevant capacities occur in the same genome. Applying the complete KEGG M00165 definition supports \CBBFull{} Calvin--Benson--Bassham (CBB) candidate MAGs, including \HtwoCBB{} with screened hydrogenase markers. Of these joint candidates, \HtwoCBBStrict{} meet the specified completeness and contamination screen and \DualHtwoCO{} also carry a form-I CO-dehydrogenase marker. The module accepts alternative regeneration routes, including 149 supported genomes with transaldolase and no annotated sedoheptulose bisphosphatase; the transaldolase variant has experimental precedent \cite{frolov2019transaldolase}.

Genome linkage changes the biological quantity that can be inferred from these observations. Given only community-level hydrogenase and CBB carrier shares, the share carrying both pathways lies in an interval. This interval has positive width in \AmbiguousSamples{} of \MappedSamples{} mapped samples, with median width 6.13 percentage points. Genome-resolved conjunction determines a specific intersection within that interval (Fig.~\ref{fig:deduction}A). A single Rubisco screen includes 1,078 MAGs, and Rubisco plus phosphoribulokinase includes 416; complete module support narrows the admitted set to \CBBFull{}. These distinctions specify the biological evidence used in subsequent calculations.

The same deduction determines which reactions enter a conditional resource calculation. A hydrogenase-plus-CBB candidate admits hydrogen-supported carbohydrate synthesis, while CO oxidation requires additional CO-carrier entailment. In a worked query with fixed substrate supplies and energy requirements, a candidate lacking that additional entailment has a carbohydrate-carbon ceiling of 0.0674~nmol~m$^{-2}$~s$^{-1}$. Admitting CO oxidation without its supporting annotation raises the computed ceiling to 0.500, a 7.42-fold change (Fig.~\ref{fig:deduction}B). The result shows how an unsupported biological correspondence changes the answer to a physical resource question. The bound is conditional on the admitted reaction network and supplied resource limits.

An independently implemented parser of the KEGG module expression reproduces every classification from the raw KO table. A flat implementation that admits reactions from tabular pathway flags instead of reasoner entailments gives the same bound, as expected from identical premises. Ontological deduction therefore contributes an explicit, checkable derivation of reaction eligibility rather than additional information. The same memberships determine the community factors of the process mechanisms (Methods), so a water addition changes water storage and uptake while the pathway classification, and every quantity deduced from it, stays fixed across regimes.

\subsection{Cross-desert tests identify transferable responses and local parameter requirements}
The process model predicts a competition between biological activation as water becomes available and reduced gas transport as water fills pores. We tested this mechanism against independent controlled hydration and field measurements from two deserts, preserving each study's measurement units and experimental support (Fig.~\ref{fig:external}). The evaluation is retrospective: published conclusions were known when the analysis protocol was fixed.

In Namib Desert microcosms, 400~$\mu$L of added water increased atmospheric hydrogen oxidation in the two fog-zone soils with quantified dry and wet rates. Mean wet-to-dry ratios were 41.3 (within-site bootstrap 95\% interval 31.1--56.0) at C14-2 and 19.4 (15.6--24.0) at C14-4. Hydrated microcosms from all ten sites reached sub-atmospheric hydrogen concentrations, while eight sites had no quantified dry atmospheric-rate endpoint within the reported observation period \cite{tribbia2026namib}. The reference model predicts the positive hydration direction at bulk densities of 1.2 and 1.5~g~cm$^{-3}$. At 1.8~g~cm$^{-3}$, hydrated C14-2 soil nearly fills the pore space, gas diffusion collapses and the predicted direction reverses, so the direction itself depends on an unmeasured soil property. It also predicts zero dry uptake at the two active fog sites, exposing a failure of those assumptions. Historical field water potentials in this study describe a different temporal and experimental support from the incubation bottles, so the observations evaluate the combined retention--activation parameterization.

The Sonoran Desert experiment provides a more discriminating response-shape test. Across four soil-source plots at one residence, increasing gravimetric water content from 0.02 to 0.20~g~g$^{-1}$ increased mean hydrogen uptake by 0.37--0.55~nmol~m$^{-2}$~s$^{-1}$. Uptake also increased from 0.10 to 0.20~g~g$^{-1}$ in every plot, by 0.32--0.51~nmol~m$^{-2}$~s$^{-1}$ \cite{buzzard2022h2uptake}. With the reference retention relation and an assumed bulk density of 1.5~g~cm$^{-3}$, the twin instead predicts a decline over this upper interval. A positive wetting response alone therefore provides an insufficient test of transferable parameter values.

We next evaluated quantitative transfer against 64 Sonoran field measurements, aggregated into 16 plot--campaign means with shared moisture and temperature observations. Holding out each entire campaign, the frozen reference model had RMSE 1.65~nmol~m$^{-2}$~s$^{-1}$. Fitting only its kinetic coefficient on the remaining campaigns gave RMSE 1.72. Allowing the soil-response parameters to vary within a fixed grid reduced error to 0.69, compared with 0.79 for the training mean and 1.35 for a linear moisture--temperature model (Table~\ref{tab:transfer}). The calibrated model improved on the training mean in three of four held campaigns, with a larger error in August. Every training split selected the no-temperature-amplification member, $Q_{10}=1$, and the largest tested porosity and retention exponent. These results identify environmental parameterization as a prerequisite for useful transfer in this one-site seasonal comparison.

\subsection{Interventions act on mechanisms while deduced quantities stay fixed}
The twin represents its definitions and mechanisms as one stratified graph (Fig.~\ref{fig:graph}). Ontological axioms and evidence determine memberships and carrier shares; constitutive maps define water content, water potential, gas diffusivity and chemical power; structural mechanisms propagate water, salt, activation, kinetic coefficients and uptake through time. Of the \GraphNodes{} represented quantities, \AdmissibleNodes{} can be intervened on: \AdmissibleInputs{} exogenous inputs (water addition, salt in added water, evaporation demand, temperature, porosity, entry potential and retention exponent) and \AdmissibleStates{} states with a physical handle (water and salt inventories). Requests to clamp uptake, a kinetic coefficient, a carrier share or a pathway membership are rejected with the zone that makes them inadmissible.

Interventions on defined quantities are translated through their definitions. Setting water content has a unique translation to water storage. Setting total water potential does not: after a 12~mm pulse, $\mathrm{do}(\psi=-1~\mathrm{MPa})$ can be realized by draining water to \PsiStrongMatricWater{}~mm or by adding salt to \PsiStrongOsmoticSalt{}~mol~L$^{-1}$ at \PsiStrongOsmoticWater{}~mm. Both versions give the same activation, \PsiStrongMatricA{}, because activation depends on potential alone, but hydrogen uptake is \PsiStrongMatricJHtwo{} and \PsiStrongOsmoticJHtwo{}~nmol~m$^{-2}$~s$^{-1}$ because water content also controls gas diffusivity. The osmotic version lies outside the dilute osmotic domain, so the twin abstains from its trajectory; at $-0.4$~MPa, where both versions are valid, three-day uptake is \PsiMildUptakeMatric{} and \PsiMildUptakeOsmotic{}~$\mu$mol~m$^{-2}$, and the ordering reverses because the osmotic version retains more water. The graph identifies this ambiguity before any simulation, from the path through water content and diffusivity that bypasses water potential.

Deduced quantities have no causal ancestors and are therefore identical in every regime. Under the deductive gate, \ClosedCOGates{} of \MappedSamples{} sampled communities contain no mapped CO-carrier genome. Their CO uptake is zero in every regime, so every water intervention has zero effect on it, a result derived from the evidence and confirmed in all \ClosedCOZeroEffects{} simulated contrasts. Every sampled community contains mapped hydrogenase carriers, so the gate leaves hydrogen scenarios unchanged. The intervention trace checks these invariants at run time, together with the subsumption inequalities between carrier shares and the constitutive identities along each trajectory. The same result is a do-calculus derivation. Ordinary $d$-separation cannot remove a water addition from CO uptake, because water content still reaches CO uptake through gas diffusivity, but conditioning on the deductive closure of a zero CO-carrier share fixes the kinetic coefficient and uptake and licenses the deletion of the intervention (Rule~3). In the same stratum, hydrogen uptake becomes independent of the CO pathway, whose oxygen demand otherwise couples the two gases. For field observations with a latent season, the effect of water storage on hydrogen uptake is identified by adjustment for the recorded water inputs, the previous water state, porosity and either temperature or evaporation demand, but no adjustment set exists when neither temperature nor evaporation is measured.

\subsection{Causal scenarios depend on intervention version and community}
The external tests motivate explicit uncertainty in the physical parameters used for Rub' al-Khali interventions. We compared equal total water additions delivered as one pulse, four pulses or daily additions over 14 days, using a shared initial state and fixed biological evidence. Subdaily integration captures uptake during the interval between wetting and drying. Daily additions totalling 12~mm yield 2.87~$\mu$mol~H$_2$~m$^{-2}$ at the reference parameters, compared with \RefHtwoFour{} for four pulses and \RefHtwoSingle{} for a single pulse; increasing integration resolution from 96 to 384 steps per day changes reference-schedule uptake by at most 0.026\%.

``Add 12~mm of water'' is therefore not a single intervention. Across \VersionMembers{} combinations of retention exponent, activation threshold, activation shape and kinetic multiplier, a single pulse exceeds four pulses in \VersionSingleFirst{} members and four pulses exceed a single pulse in \VersionFourFirst{} (Fig.~\ref{fig:scenarios}A). Daily additions never exceed either. The effect of a single pulse relative to the untreated control ranges from \VersionEffectSingleLow{} to \VersionEffectSingleHigh{}~$\mu$mol~m$^{-2}$ across the grid. The control belongs to every evaluated Pareto set because it uses no added water or disturbance, while the preferred wetting schedule depends on the parameter member. The twin preserves this range and identifies pulse timing and soil response as quantities required to resolve a management comparison.

Coupling the mechanisms to sampled communities makes the scenarios site-specific. Under the explicit assumption that the hydrogen rate coefficient scales with the mapped hydrogenase-carrier share, the single-pulse effect has median \HtwoPropSingleMedianDeep{}~$\mu$mol~m$^{-2}$ in subsurface sand, \HtwoPropSingleMedianSurface{} in surface sand and \HtwoPropSingleMedianRoot{} in root-adjacent soil (Fig.~\ref{fig:scenarios}B). In matched site--campaign comparisons, subsurface sand exceeds root-adjacent soil by \ContrastDeepRootEst{}~$\mu$mol~m$^{-2}$ (site-bootstrap 95\% interval \ContrastDeepRootLow{}--\ContrastDeepRootHigh{}; \ContrastDeepRootVisits{} visits). These differences follow entirely from the proportionality assumption; under the deductive gate they vanish. They identify carrier-share-resolved uptake measurements as the test that would distinguish the two couplings.

\section{Methods}
\subsection{A stratified causal--ontological model}
The twin separates three kinds of change. An observation update changes the evidence $E$ (annotations, marker assignments, mapping weights). A model revision changes the ontology $K$ or a mechanism. An intervention changes exogenous physical inputs while $E$ and $K$ remain fixed. Selecting a sampled community conditions the twin on evidence and is not an intervention.

Following the treatment of non-causal constraints in structural causal models \cite{bochman2015pearls,pearl2009causality}, the model has two zones. The invariant zone contains the OWL axioms $K$, the deduced memberships $D_K(E)$ and constitutive maps $\Phi$: definitions such as $\theta=W/(1000L)$, $W_{\max}=1000L\phi$, the retention and osmotic relations, total potential, Millington--Quirk diffusivity, carrier shares, chemical power and the declared observation maps. The causal zone contains structural mechanisms for water storage $W_t$, salt inventory $N_t$, activation $A_t$, the kinetic coefficient $k_{g,t}$ and uptake $J_{g,t}$. Exogenous inputs are daily water addition, NaCl in added water, evaporation demand, temperature and soil parameters. The causal graph has an edge from each argument of a mechanism or constitutive map to its output and is acyclic when unrolled over time (Fig.~\ref{fig:graph}); the executable graph records the zone, unit, equation and manipulability of every node.

Deduction enters the mechanisms through a community factor $\gamma_g$ for gas $g$. For a sampled community $s$, $q_s(C)$ is the mapped-read share of MAGs that the reasoner entails to belong to class $C$, and
\begin{equation}
k_{g,t}=k_{g,\mathrm{ref}}\,\gamma_g\,A(\psi_t)\,Q_{10}^{(T_t-T_{\mathrm{ref}})/10},\qquad
\gamma_g\in\Bigl\{1,\;\mathbf{1}[q_s(C_g)>0],\;\frac{q_s(C_g)}{\operatorname{median}_{s'}q_{s'}(C_g)}\Bigr\},
\end{equation}
with $C_{\mathrm{H_2}}$ the hydrogenase-carrier class and $C_{\mathrm{CO}}$ the form-I CO-dehydrogenase carrier class. The three options are the reference community, a deductive gate and an explicitly assumed proportionality between carrier share and rate coefficient. The reference scenarios use $\gamma_g=1$. The same memberships admit reactions to the resource ceiling.

An intervention $\mathrm{do}(X_I=x_I)$ replaces the mechanisms or input values of the targets $I$ and leaves every other mechanism, every constitutive map, $K$, $E$ and the initial state unchanged. It is admissible when every target is an exogenous input or a causal state with a physical handle (water or salt inventory at the start of a day) and some state satisfies all constitutive maps and unsevered mechanisms; for example, a reduced porosity that makes initial water exceed pore capacity is rejected. Deduced memberships, carrier shares, constituted quantities and the outcome mechanisms $A$, $k$ and $J$ cannot be clamped. A target in the invariant zone is defined through its constituents. Setting water content, $\mathrm{do}(\theta=\theta^*)$, translates uniquely to $\mathrm{do}(W=1000L\theta^*)$. Setting total water potential is an ambiguous manipulation \cite{spirtes2004ambiguous}: matric and osmotic versions reach the same $\psi$ by different water and salt inventories. Its effect on activation is version-independent because $A$ depends only on $\psi$, whereas its effect on uptake depends on the version through the path $W\to\theta\to D_g\to J_g$ that bypasses $\psi$. The implementation derives such version relevance from the graph and requires a version for ambiguous targets. Likewise, a fixed total water addition over a horizon is realized by several schedules, and its effect is defined only for a stated schedule or mixture of schedules \cite{vanderweele2013multiple}.

Three consequences follow from the construction and are checked at run time. First, $D_K(E)$, $q_s$ and $\gamma_g$ have no causal ancestors, so they are identical in every admissible regime, and every entailed subsumption lifts to the shares; for example, the share of hydrogenase-plus-CBB candidates never exceeds the hydrogenase-carrier share. Second, constitutive identities hold along every intervened trajectory. Third, if $\gamma_g=0$ for a community, uptake of gas $g$ is zero in every regime, so every interventional contrast on that uptake is zero by deduction. A measured uptake in such a community would show that the evidence missed a carrier, not that a definition is false, because absence of entailment is not asserted as biological absence. Paired regimes share the initial state, non-targeted inputs, parameters and evidence, so their difference is a unit-level counterfactual contrast within the deterministic model.

Deduction also enters identification. For an observed stratum $\mathbf{Z}=\mathbf{z}$, the deductive value closure $\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbf{Z}=\mathbf{z})$ contains every quantity whose value is entailed by the definitions together with the mechanisms of quantities that no admissible intervention can replace; because these mechanisms are never severed, the entailed values hold in every regime. The closure determines a constitutive quantity when all its arguments are known, and propagates zeros: $q_s(C_g)=0$ entails $\gamma_g=0$, $k_g=0$ and $J_g=0$, and $\psi\leq\psi_d$ entails $A=0$. Following the context-specific extension of $d$-separation to deterministic and ontologically determined nodes \cite{geiger1990identifying}, the rules of the $\mathrm{do}$-calculus are evaluated on the time-unrolled graph with the conditioning set $\mathbf{Z}\cup\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbf{Z}=\mathbf{z})$, after removing edges whose mechanism is inert in the stratum, and an outcome fixed by the closure is treated as a constant. Back-door adjustment sets for field data are searched over observed non-descendants of the treatment in a graph with a latent season that drives water input, temperature and evaporation (Suppl. Section~S3).

These interventional quantities are derived from the stated mechanisms; the observational metagenomes do not identify them. Compartment contrasts and forecasts are associational. The external hydration microcosms set water content experimentally and therefore test interventional predictions, whereas field campaigns vary moisture and temperature jointly with season and test the mechanism observationally. Transfer between deserts is a transportability problem in which soil parameters and activation thresholds may differ between environments \cite{pearl2014external}; calibration re-estimates exactly these mechanisms. Because $K$ and $\Phi$ are definitional, a failed process prediction implicates a mechanism, an assumed parameter or an observation map.

For carrier sets $A$ and $B$ measured on the same reference universe, their marginal shares imply
\begin{equation}
\max\{0,q_i(A)+q_i(B)-1\}\leq q_i(A\cap B)\leq\min\{q_i(A),q_i(B)\}.
\end{equation}
Genome-resolved annotations determine the intersection. Observation maps preserve the endpoint, unit, denominator and experimental support of each quantity. The ontology declares measurement endpoints as disjoint classes and records which conversions are defined: a first-order coefficient converts to bottle uptake given headspace gas amount and soil mass, gravimetric to volumetric water content requires bulk density, and no direct map relates a bottle rate to an areal flux without geometric and transport assumptions.

\subsection{Evidence inputs and module semantics}
The primary programme sampled surface soil, bulk subsurface soil at 5--10~cm and root-adjacent soil at the same depth. Soil was frozen in the field and stored for molecular analysis. The March 2023 field ledger records site coordinates, collection dates, biome and local features; the 59 core-site reference coordinates in this cohort match that ledger. Metagenomic assemblies were binned, refined and dereplicated into species representatives, and genes on contigs removed during refinement were excluded from the representative annotations. The twin's molecular analysis starts from 551 metagenomes, \ReferenceMAGs{} reference MAGs, corrected post-refinement KO annotations, MAG quality and GTDB taxonomy, trace-gas marker assignments, CoverM mapping percentages, read-screen genome equivalents and curated collection dates. The versioned input snapshot includes each of these analysis inputs and checksums. Sequencing, assembly and primary annotation precede this analysis boundary. Genome equivalents use the screened marker-gene read counts and single-copy-gene normalizer, with a 60\% identity floor. They denote relative gene-copy capacity.

For mapped-read weight $w_{ig}$ of MAG $g$ in sample $i$, the share of a carrier set $A$ within reference set $G$ is
\begin{equation}
q_i(A)=\frac{\sum_{g\in A}w_{ig}}{\sum_{g\in G}w_{ig}}.
\end{equation}
This share is unavailable when the denominator is zero or any required mapping weight is nonfinite. The evidence builder checks unique sample--MAG pairs, nonnegative weights, reference membership and closure of mapped plus unmapped percentages to $100\pm0.02\%$. Mapped-MAG Shannon entropy uses the same normalized weights. Genome equivalents, read shares, DNA recovery, membrane integrity, biomass and activity are separate endpoints \cite{carini2016relicdna,lennon2018relicdna}.

The pathway rule implements the 11 conjunctive requirements of KEGG M00165 as retrieved on 28 October 2026. Alternatives within a requirement are disjunctive; K01602 is optional in the module definition. The regeneration branch accepts K01100, K11532 or K01086 for the bisphosphatase route, or K00616 or K13810 for the transaldolase route. The grouping expresses alternative support for the module requirement; the two routes retain their distinct biochemical meanings. A separate parser evaluates the original KEGG expression, including parentheses, alternative branches, complexes and optional components, against the full source KO table without importing the production step dictionary.

We encode each MAG as an OWL class with existential restrictions for its observed KOs and marker guilds. The CBB-support class is the intersection of the requirement classes. Hydrogenase and CO carrier classes derive from the respective marker hierarchies; their intersections with CBB support define candidate eligibility. The declared quality subset requires completeness at least 90\% and contamination below 5\%, without claiming the complete MIMAG high-quality criteria. Domain assertions and a coarse four-bucket phylum partition support the exported ontology; detailed GTDB taxonomic summaries are tabular annotations rather than a complete imported taxonomic hierarchy.

Each MAG class is a subclass of $\exists\,\mathsf{hasKO}.K$ for every KEGG orthology group $K$ to which at least one of its genes is assigned. Pathway, enzyme, chemical and environment classes are linked to the Gene Ontology, ChEBI, ENVO, PATO and the Units of Measurement Ontology by annotation mappings rather than subsumption, because a genome class is not a process or function class; every mapped term was checked against the Ontology Lookup Service on 30 October 2026. Measurement endpoints are pairwise disjoint subclasses of the SIO measurement-value class, and declared observation-map classes record the assumptions each conversion requires. Kobayashi-MaRust v1.5.3 classifies the OWL ontology and produces verified subset-minimal justifications for every hydrogenase-plus-CBB candidate and every CO-carrier MAG, and for the disjointness of every pair of measurement endpoints. The exported membership artifact records the ontology, evidence, rules and reasoner identities. Before memberships are used, the runtime verifies the artifact checksums, the rule and evidence digests, the digest of the shipped ontology and the pinned reasoner digest. Missing entailment remains an admission decision under incomplete annotations. Numerical aggregation and conservation checks are implemented separately from OWL subsumption.

\subsection{Conditional regimes, water and salt balance}
The state $(W_t,N_t)$ comprises water storage in mm and NaCl inventory in mol~m$^{-2}$; daily forcing and soil parameters are the exogenous inputs of the preceding section.

For layer depth $L$ and porosity $\phi$, pore capacity is $W_{\max}=1000L\phi$~mm. At the start of a day, water $I$ with NaCl concentration $c_I$ adds $I$ to water and $Ic_I$ to salt; overflow removes a proportionate amount of the mixed salt. The day is divided into $n=96$ substeps. Each substep drains
\begin{equation}
D=\left[1-(1-d)^{1/n}\right]\max(0,W-fW_{\max}),
\end{equation}
where $d$ is the daily drainage fraction and $f$ the field-capacity fraction. Drainage removes $ND/W$ salt when $W>0$, followed by evaporation $\min(\mathrm{Ev}/n,W-D)$ without salt removal. Gas rates are evaluated at the midpoint of the pre- and post-substep water and salt inventories and integrated over $86400/n$ seconds. We compare 24, 96 and 384 substeps for the four reference schedules. The complete defaults and assumptions are generated from the executable contract in Suppl. Sections~S1--S3.

Volumetric water content is $\theta=W/(1000L)$. Campbell retention gives matric potential $\psi_m=\psi_e(\theta/\phi)^{-b}$ for entry potential $\psi_e<0$ and exponent $b>0$ \cite{campbell1974retention}. In dilute NaCl, osmotic potential is $\psi_o=-2\varphi cRT\times10^{-3}$~MPa, where $c$ is mol~L$^{-1}$, $\varphi$ the osmotic coefficient, $R$ the gas constant and $T$ absolute temperature. Substeps outside the specified dilute domain, default 0.1~mol~L$^{-1}$, or with salt in dry soil make daily and integrated gas outputs unavailable. For total potential $\psi=\psi_m+\psi_o$, activation rises from zero at $\psi_d$ to one at $\psi_w$:
\begin{equation}
A(\psi)=\left[\frac{\log|\psi_d/\psi|}{\log|\psi_d/\psi_w|}\right]^a
\quad(\psi_d<\psi<\psi_w),
\end{equation}
with zero and one outside the ramp and shape exponent $a>0$. Thresholds and the ramp require local physiological evaluation.

\subsection{Gas exchange and resource admission}
Millington--Quirk diffusivity is $D_g=D_{0,g}(\phi-\theta)^{10/3}/\phi^2$ \cite{millington1961permeability}. For reference reaction coefficient $k_{g,\mathrm{ref}}$, temperature response $Q_{10}$ and reference temperature $T_{\mathrm{ref}}$, we set $k_g=k_{g,\mathrm{ref}}A(\psi)\allowbreak Q_{10}^{(T-T_{\mathrm{ref}})/10}$. A homogeneous first-order layer with atmospheric concentration $C_{a,g}$ at the surface and zero basal concentration gradient has uptake
\begin{equation}
J_g=C_{a,g}\sqrt{D_gk_g}\tanh\left(L\sqrt{k_g/D_g}\right).
\end{equation}
Hydrogen and CO oxidation consume half a mole of oxygen per mole of donor. A prescribed oxygen limit scales their fluxes proportionally when required. Chemical power is $202000J_{\mathrm{H_2}}+243000J_{\mathrm{CO}}$~W~m$^{-2}$, multiplied by a supplied capture fraction. Reaction coefficients are independent inputs rather than conversions from metagenomic counts.

The separate resource ceiling maximizes carbohydrate-carbon flux $v_C$ using hydrogen respiration $v_H$ and CO respiration $v_{CO}$. For hydrogen, CO, oxygen and external carbon-dioxide supply bounds $H,C_O,O,C_{\mathrm{ext}}$, capture fraction $\eta$, maintenance demand $M$ and synthesis cost $\epsilon_C$, constraints are
\begin{align}
v_H+2v_C&\leq H,& v_{CO}&\leq C_O,\\
(v_H+v_{CO})/2&\leq O,& v_C-v_{CO}&\leq C_{\mathrm{ext}},\\
\eta(202000v_H+243000v_{CO})&\geq M+\epsilon_Cv_C.
\end{align}
The underlying balanced reactions are hydrogen oxidation, CO oxidation and $2\mathrm{H_2}+\mathrm{CO_2}\rightarrow\mathrm{CH_2O}+\mathrm{H_2O}$. All fluxes are nonnegative. The query requires entailed hydrogenase-plus-CBB support and sets $v_{CO}=0$ without CO-carrier entailment. HiGHS solves the scaled linear programme, with post-solution elemental and constraint residual checks \cite{huangfu2018highs}. The ceiling uses supplied flux bounds and is not added to realized process uptake. Ecosystem carbon removal is a separate measured stock-change account requiring imports, geological carbon, counterfactual change and lifecycle emissions.

Scenario comparison crosses $b\in\{3,5\}$, $\psi_d\in\{-70,-100\}$~MPa, $a\in\{0.5,2\}$ and kinetic multipliers $\{0.1,1,10\}$. The four schedules use a 14-day horizon, initial water 1.8~mm, temperature $25~^\circ$C and evaporation 1~mm~day$^{-1}$. Three schedules add 12~mm as one pulse, four pulses or daily pulses; the control adds none. Pulses fall on days 0, 3, 7 and 10 for the four-pulse schedule and on day 0 for the single pulse. Pareto evaluation maximizes hydrogen uptake and minimizes added water, remaining salt and prescribed disturbed area, set to 1~m$^2$ for each wetting schedule and zero for the control. The grid is a sensitivity design, not a probability distribution.

\subsection{Prediction and observational evaluation}
Biological replicates are averaged within site--compartment--campaign visits. Ridge regression with penalty 10 predicts $\log(1+\mathrm{GE})$ from training-standardized latitude and longitude, annual sine and cosine of collection day and one-hot compartment. Baselines are global mean, compartment mean and the latest site--compartment observation, with compartment/global fallback at unseen locations. Each test campaign uses training observations collected before its first sample. The reference annotation catalogue is fixed retrospectively across all campaigns.

Nominal 90\% intervals use absolute residuals from up to five site-held-out training folds, with empirical quantile level $\min\{1,\lceil(n+1)0.9\rceil/n\}$ for $n$ training visits. These pooled cross-validation residuals centre intervals on a refit model; dependence and temporal shift preclude a distribution-free coverage guarantee. Persistence uses its unseen-site fallback within these calibration folds. We report empirical coverage by campaign and compartment. Paired model-error comparisons resample sites with all their visits, using 2,000 percentile-bootstrap draws and seed 42. Matched ecological contrasts use the same resampling unit after averaging replicates within site and campaign.

\subsection{External datasets, observation maps and validation splits}
We retrieved the primary supplementary data for Buzzard et al. and Tribbia et al. through the PMC public cloud service and verified their publisher-supplied checksums \cite{buzzard2022h2uptake,tribbia2026namib}. The Sonoran analysis uses the authors' corrected response-factor column, with sign reversed so that uptake is positive. Controlled incubation data comprise four source batches within each of four plots at three moisture levels (48 bottles). Hydration contrasts match the source batch, and uncertainty resamples the four source batches within each plot (5,000 draws). The four plots belong to one residence. Gravimetric-to-volumetric conversion uses an explicitly assumed bulk density, with sensitivity values 1.2, 1.5 and 1.8~g~cm$^{-3}$.

Sonoran field data comprise four collars per plot at four campaigns. Because collars within a plot--campaign share the sensor-derived environmental inputs, predictions are evaluated against 16 plot--campaign means. Each test fold holds out an entire campaign. A bounded scalar fit selects the kinetic coefficient on training observations. The soil-response calibration additionally searches porosity $\{0.35,0.40,0.50\}$, retention exponent $\{2,4,6\}$, entry potentials of $-0.0001$, $-0.001$ and $-0.01$~MPa and $Q_{10}\in\{1,2\}$, selecting the lowest training mean squared error. The kinetic search is bounded between $10^{-9}$ and $10^2$~s$^{-1}$. The diffusion-only comparator fits its coefficient on identical training data. A training mean and ordinary least-squares model with intercept, moisture and temperature supply empirical baselines. Errors are descriptive for the four-campaign dataset; campaign holdout includes interpolation across seasons.

Namib supplementary tables provide 50 wet and ten dry atmospheric oxidation rates. Dry rates are quantified for two of the ten sites. The remaining dry endpoints remain unquantified, consistent with the reported failure to reach sub-atmospheric concentrations during the observation period. Wet and dry assays use separate subsamples, so fold-change intervals independently resample five observations per condition within each site. The conversion from the first-order coefficient to mass-normalized uptake uses the reported headspace gas amount, atmospheric hydrogen mixing ratio and 2~g soil mass. Gravimetric soil moisture uses the workbook's percentage cell format. Historical field water potential and climatological soil-moisture estimates are excluded as direct measurements of bottle conditions.

For the temperate comparison, we fit $C(t)=C(0)e^{-kt}$ to each of 77 dilution-corrected bottle time series, retaining negative fitted rates. A nonnegative common amplitude multiplies either the air-pore diffusion ratio or its product with activation at $\psi_d=-85$~MPa. Each fold fits two soils and predicts the third. The threshold was informed by the same study. The previously reported within-soil, held-replicate evaluation is retained as a development comparison. All external tests preserve native endpoint units, and source files, exclusions, parameter fits and row-level predictions are released. The retrospective evaluation protocol was written after reading the source studies and before numerical fitting.

\section{Discussion}
EQ-BioDT connects primary observations from the Rub' al-Khali to an explicit account of biological capacity and the consequences of physical interventions. Ontological deduction resolves pathway conjunctions, controls reaction admission and sets community factors in the rate mechanisms. Causal mechanisms propagate specified water, salt and pore changes, and the separation of zones determines which interventions are admissible, which effects follow by deduction and which interventions require a stated version. The resulting twin represents microbial processes across a repeated sampling programme in the world's largest continuous sand desert, with an observation footprint and computational assumptions that can be inspected independently.

The external tests reveal the value of keeping these components distinct. Hydration responses transfer in direction across the Namib and Sonoran settings, but the default retention--activation parameterization misses dry activity and the upper-moisture response. Adjusting a rate coefficient alone leaves large Sonoran field errors; environmental calibration provides a substantially better fit to held campaigns. The consistent selection of boundary grid values also identifies unresolved parameter sensitivity. Between temperate soils, the activation envelope transfers successfully to two environments and poorly to a third. These results support a model of environmentally contingent microbial responses and locate the measurements needed to improve it.

The deductive contribution is expressed through admissible biological interpretations and conditional bounds. The same-information flat implementation agrees with the ontology-based calculation, as expected from their common premises; the ontology adds no predictive information. Its contributions are explicit definitions and derivations, the separation of invariant definitions from severable mechanisms, deductive zero effects and declared measurement correspondences. The stratification also localizes failures. Definitions cannot be refuted by an uptake measurement, so the failed Namib dry-activity prediction implicates the activation threshold and retention relation, and a measured CO uptake in a community without mapped CO carriers would implicate incomplete evidence rather than the pathway definitions. This is particularly relevant when transferring models: field-climatological moisture, incubation water content, genomic capacity and measured uptake have distinct experimental meanings. An executable connection between those meanings and causal mechanisms provides a foundation for extending the twin as new measurements become available.

The present implementation represents genomic observations and conditional soil processes. The proportional coupling between carrier share and rate coefficient is an untested assumption, and the parameter grid is a sensitivity design rather than a probability distribution over interventional outcomes. Continuous field-state assimilation and local flux calibration remain development requirements. The represented geographic extent follows the primary sampling footprint, and inference beyond that footprint requires spatial validation. Assembly incompleteness and enzyme specificity affect pathway support; genome-resolved transcript, protein and isotope observations would test how the deduced capacities are expressed. Controlled Rub' al-Khali wetting experiments with measured retention curves and simultaneous hydrogen uptake would directly test the environmental parameters implicated by the external analyses. Forecasts frozen before further sampling would add prospective evidence to the repeated-campaign evaluation.
