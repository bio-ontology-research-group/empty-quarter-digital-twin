This note states the constitutive axioms of the OWL 2 constraint module rubalkhali_twin_
constraints.owl (version 0.2.0) of the Rub’ al-Khali ontology, from which the constitutive layer
Φconst of EQ-BioDT is compiled, the measurement assumptions under which they become linear
constraints on measured quantities, and the constraint set C(Tont ) that these constraints define.
It then proves the three results used in the main text: the error guarantee of the projection
onto C(Tont ) (Theorem 2), the bounds on interventions on coarse classes under a linear allocation
model (Proposition 1), and the relation between sibling-class effects and the effect on their parent
(Proposition 2).

S1.1      Constitutive axioms
The constitutive layer uses five disjoint-union axioms of Tont . In OWL 2, the axiom
DisjointUnion(D, C1 , . . . , Ck ) states that D is equivalent to C1 ⊔ · · · ⊔ Ck and that the Ci are
pairwise disjoint [1]. Written in description logic notation, with the class identifiers of the
module in parentheses, the axioms are:
(A1) Prokaryote ≡ Actinomycetota ⊔ Thermoplasmatota ⊔ Halobacteriota ⊔ OtherProkaryote,
     pairwise disjoint (RAK_TAXON_TOTAL_PROKARYOTA; NCBITaxon:201174,
     NCBITaxon:2283796 and NCBITaxon:3100166 for the three phyla; RAK_TAXON_
     OTHER_PROKARYOTA).
(A2) MeasuredNiFeHydrogenase ≡ Group1l ⊔ Group1h ⊔ Group2a, pairwise disjoint (RAK_
     GUILD_TOTAL_H2ASE; RAK_GUILD_HYDB_1L, RAK_GUILD_HYDB_1H and
     RAK_GUILD_HYDB_2A). The parent class is the union of the three measured sub-
     groups.
(A3) TraceGasScavengerCapacity ≡ MeasuredNiFeHydrogenase ⊔ FormICoxL, disjoint (RAK_
     GUILD_TOTAL_TRACE_GAS; RAK_GUILD_COXL_FORMI).

Each disjoint-union axiom entails Ci ⊑ D for each of its members, and the module states all 13
of these subsumptions as SubClassOf axioms: the four members of (A1) under Prokaryote, the
three subgroups under MeasuredNiFeHydrogenase, MeasuredNiFeHydrogenase and FormICoxL
under TraceGasScavengerCapacity, the two oxidation fluxes under AtmosphericTraceGasPower ,
and the two DNA pools under TotalSoilDNA. An annotation property binds each of the 17 classes
to a state variable of the twin, and a second annotation marks the eight classes whose quantities
are relative abundances or DNA fractions with the upper bound 1. EQ-BioDT compiles the
constraints below from these axioms and annotations each time the constitutive layer is built,
and each constraint carries the axiom it implements.

S1.2    Measurement semantics
The axioms constrain classes. To obtain constraints on measured quantities, each variable yC of
a state is read as the value µ(C) of a quantity defined on the extension of class C in a soil sample,
with one measure and one normalization for each partition: for (A2) and (A3), the number of
gene copies of the class divided by the number of genome equivalents in the sample; for (A1),
the fraction of 16S rRNA reads assigned to the class; for (A4), the oxidation power contributed
by the reactions of the class; and for (A5), the fraction of soil DNA in the pool. Gene copies of
disjoint enzyme classes are additive under a common denominator, so (A2) and (A3) constrain
copies per genome equivalent; organisms that carry several of these genes contribute to several
classes. The intact-cell DNA pool of (A5) is operationally defined by the two-pool model of the
twin and includes DNA in intact cells irrespective of their viability.

S1.3     Constraint projection
Theorem 1. C(Tont ) is a nonempty, closed and convex set. For every ŷ ∈ Rp , the Euclidean
projection ΠC(Tont ) (ŷ) = arg minz∈C(Tont ) ∥z − ŷ∥2 exists and is unique.

Proof. C(Tont ) is the intersection of finitely many closed half-spaces and hyperplanes, so it is
closed and convex, and 0 ∈ C(Tont ). The function z 7→ ∥z − ŷ∥22 is continuous, coercive and
strictly convex, so it attains its minimum on the nonempty closed set C(Tont ), and strict convexity
together with convexity of C(Tont ) makes the minimizer unique.

    Corollary 1 follows by summing Theorem 2 over samples. Theorem 2 is a standard property
of metric projection onto a closed convex set, and it bounds the total squared error of a vector; in-
dividual coordinates, rank correlations and quantities computed downstream from the projected
state can still change in either direction. In EQ-BioDT, the theorem applies to the projection
step of Φconst , whose input is the preprocessed state, and it compares the projected state with
that input; the preceding truncation, phylum rescaling and clipping are heuristic operations.
In Tier 3, the observed aggregates are sums of the observed subgroup GE, so each of the 550
observed vectors lies in C(Tont ) and Theorem 2 applies sample by sample; the observed improve-
ment of every sample in Tier 3 therefore follows from the construction of the benchmark, and the
size of the improvement, measured against reconciliation baselines, is the empirical result. The
projection is computed numerically by sequential least-squares quadratic programming, and the
per-sample check uses a tolerance of 10−7 . The disjoint-union constraints are linear in GE and
become nonlinear after a logarithmic transform, so Tier 3 predicts and projects untransformed
GE. In the scenario engines, the aggregates are recomputed by the preprocessing step before
projection, so OCS = 1 for scenario states verifies the constitutive layer of each run.

S2.1    Synthetic metagenome benchmark
All guild abundances in the state cube are estimated from reads, so the accuracy of the twin
depends on how well the read-to-guild pipeline recovers known abundances. In the synthetic
metagenome benchmark (b3_mock_sim.py), 20 synthetic communities were constructed from
paired subsurface bulk and root-adjacent CoverM relative-abundance profiles across 5,438 derepli-
cated Rub’ al-Khali MAG representatives (contigs ≥ 150 nt), stratified across expeditions; 150-nt
reads were drawn in proportion to each genome’s relative abundance with Poisson(0.75) base
substitutions (0.5% per base) and truncated to 100 nt (seqtk trimfq -L 100) to match the
empiricalPread-mapping
                    P workflow, with true GE defined as the cell-weighted mean marker copy
number        c m
            g g g /    g cg (cg = RAg /Lg ). Evaluated with the full reference database at the
60% identity floor, estimated and true GE correlated on the log scale with Pearson r between
0.9314 (coxL_formI) and 0.9976 (hydB_1l) and Spearman ρ between 0.9404 and 0.9752 across
17 to 19 synthetic metagenomes per guild (Table S1). Estimates exceeded true GE by a median
of 0.60 to 0.84 log2 units, a 1.5- to 1.8-fold overestimate. In eight synthetic root-versus-bulk
pairs, the estimated hydB_1l contrasts matched the true contrasts with RMSE 0.2277 log2 units,
R2 = 0.9888 and ρ = 1.0000. The benchmark therefore supports the rank-based accuracy met-
rics of Tiers 1 and 2 and the fold-change contrasts of Tier 4 for compositions represented in the
synthetic communities. Validation (Tiers 1–3), the state cube and the scenario baselines all use
GE at the 60% identity floor of this benchmark; the overestimate translates into GE-to-power
conversions in proportion, and it cancels in a contrast where the bias is matched between the
two conditions. The validation suite reads the benchmark’s summary tables, and regeneration
of the read-to-guild evaluation requires the companion workflow.

S3.1    Alignment with European biodiversity digital twins
EQ-BioDT adopts the data and provenance conventions of three European biodiversity digital
twin initiatives so that its inputs and outputs can be exchanged with their infrastructures. From
BioDT (https://biodt.eu) it takes the representation of datasets and model runs as FAIR
Digital Objects described by RO-Crate metadata, and the achievement scalarizing functions of
the DESDEO framework for multi-objective decision support [3], which EQ-BioDT evaluates
directly for its six candidate portfolios. From B-Cubed (https://b-cubed.eu) it takes the
occurrence cube, which aggregates occurrences over taxonomic, temporal and gridded spatial
dimensions [4]. From LTER-LIFE (https://lter-life.nl) and the OBIS community it takes
the Darwin Core Archive with an Event core and ExtendedMeasurementOrFact extension for
site-based monitoring data [5].

S3.2    Darwin Core Archive
The archive consists of event.txt, occurrence.txt, emof.txt, the structural descriptor
meta.xml and Ecological Metadata Language v2.2.0 metadata in eml.xml. meta.xml de-
clares the Event file as core (row type http://rs.tdwg.org/dwc/terms/Event) and the
Occurrence (http://rs.tdwg.org/dwc/terms/Occurrence) and ExtendedMeasurementOrFact
(http://rs.iobis.org/obis/terms/ExtendedMeasurementOrFact) files as extensions linked
to the core through eventID.
    The Event core holds one event for each of the 180 site-compartment states. Its eventID
has the form RAK_SITE_nn_COMPARTMENT and its parentEventID the form RAK_SITE_nn,
which groups the events of one site. The locationID is the site IRI in the Rub’ al-Khali
knowledge base, eventDate is the period of the five campaigns as the ISO 8601 interval 2023-
03-17/2025-10-15, decimalLatitude, decimalLongitude and the elevation fields give the site

position, habitat lists the ENVO classes of the desert habitat [6], samplingProtocol holds
the IRI of the compartment class (surface, 5–10 cm subsurface or rhizosphere sample), and
fieldNotes records the compartment, H3 resolution 6 cell and ERA5 cell.
    The Occurrence extension holds five occurrences for each of the 155 events with metagenomes
(775 in total): Actinomycetota, Thermoplasmatota and Halobacteriota with taxonRank
phylum and organismQuantityType relative abundance fraction, and the group 1l [NiFe]-
hydrogenase and form I CO dehydrogenase guilds with taxonRank functional guild and
organismQuantityType copies per genome equivalent. occurrenceStatus is present when
organismQuantity is positive (658 occurrences) and absent otherwise (117), and basisOfRecord
is MaterialSample. The phylum quantities are the MAG phylum shares and the guild quantities
the observed GE of the state, both from the unimputed columns of the cube.
    The ExtendedMeasurementOrFact extension holds 15 measurements per event (2,700 in to-
tal): XRF concentrations of the 11 core elements and vanadium in percent, CaCl2 pH, 16S
rRNA Shannon diversity and mean annual precipitation, the last with the UO millimetre unit
and the precipitation-value type of rubalkhali.owl. For XRF analytes, measurementTypeID is
the analyte-specific quality IRI of rubalkhali.owl, measurementValueID the ChEBI IRI of the
element [7], and measurementUnitID the UO IRI of percent.

S3.3    B-Cubed occurrence cube
The B-Cubed cube holds one row per observed state and taxon or guild (1,240 rows for the
155 states with metagenomes and eight taxa or guilds): Actinomycetota, Thermoplasmatota,
Halobacteriota and Chloroflexota, the group 1l and 1h [NiFe]-hydrogenase guilds, the form I CO
dehydrogenase guild, and the desert virome. Its dimensions are the campaign period, the H3
cells at resolutions 5 and 6, the ERA5 cell, the site and its IRI, the compartment, the transect
zone, and the taxon key, scientific name and rank (phylum, functional guild or viral guild). Its
measures are the number of metagenomes of the state as occurrence count, the unimputed relative
abundance, GE or viral richness, Shannon diversity H ′ , Hill diversity of order 1, exp(H ′ ), and
a minimum coordinate uncertainty of 10 m. A companion indicator table aggregates Shannon
and Hill diversity, observed guild GE, viral richness, MAG phylum shares, evaporite index, pH
and mean annual precipitation per H3 resolution 5 cell, transect zone and compartment. A
Frictionless tabular data package (datapackage.json) describes both tables, declares the H3
grid at resolutions 5 and 6 in EPSG:4326, and lists the field types of the cube.

S3.4    FAIR Digital Objects and Workflow Run RO-Crates
Every simulation and every portfolio comparison produces a JSON-LD RO-Crate [8] with the
RO-Crate 1.1 context. Its metadata descriptor declares conformance to RO-Crate 1.1 and to the
Process Run Crate profile v0.4 (https://w3id.org/ro/wfrun/process/0.4). The root dataset
names the run and mentions a CreateAction entity, which records the intervention and site,
start and end times, a completed action status, the path of the engine module as instrument,
the site IRI as location, each parameter setting passed to the engine as a PropertyValue object,
and a result dataset that carries the output summary and the ontological consistency score of
the run.
    Identifiers are derived from content. EQ-BioDT serializes the software version (eq-biodt
v0.2.0), intervention name, site IRI, parameters, outputs, the SHA-256 digest of the OWL
constraint module rubalkhali_twin_constraints.owl, and the SHA-256 digests of the
processed state cubes as JSON with sorted keys and computes its SHA-256 digest; the run
identifier is the version 5 UUID of this digest, and the FAIR Digital Object identifier is
https://rubalkhali.science/fdo/eq-biodt/ followed by the first 16 hexadecimal digits
of the digest. Two runs with identical software version, input cubes, settings and outputs
therefore receive the same identifier, and any change of version, input cube, parameter or output


                                                8
produces a new one. The identifiers are generated locally, and resolving them requires deposition
of the crates. The FAIR Digital Object kernel metadata returned with each run record its
identifier, the BioDT FDO profile, the software version, the checksum, the constraint-module
and state-cube SHA-256 digests, the creation time, the ontological consistency score and the
Proposition 1 bounds of the run, and the root dataset and the instrument of the crate carry
the software version. The portfolio comparison summarized in Table S5 is recorded in the
same way, with the objective weights and the number of evaluated portfolios as parameters and
the highest-ranked portfolio, its objective scores and the size of the Pareto front as outputs.

Supplementary Note 4: Assumptions and operational scope
This note distinguishes the sources of numerical values from the assumptions that permit their
interpretation. Measured quantities comprise sequence-derived gene capacity, mapped-read
composition, amplicon diversity, XRF concentrations, pH and the sampled water chemistry.
Environmental products and companion analyses supply additional covariates and calibrated
contrasts. Response coefficients introduced to translate these quantities into management
effects are assumptions unless a matching fitted estimate and endpoint are specified. The
accompanying machine-readable coefficient inventory lists numeric expressions, defaults
and clipping thresholds in the executable model with their source locations; the inventory
records implementation choices and does not establish empirical support. It is supplied as
reproducibility/outputs/model_coefficient_inventory.json.

Observation, preprocessing and prediction
The cube averages heterogeneous samples within each site and compartment and therefore treats
the resulting mean as a representative baseline. MAG shares condition on mapped reads, and
marker-gene GE represents genomic capacity. The three RuBisCO/CBB marker screens have
different sensitivities to pathway type, annotation and genome completeness. Inferring realized
oxidation or fixation additionally requires active biomass, expression, substrate delivery and per-
cell kinetics. DNA extraction yield is used as a biomass proxy in the revegetation calculation,
which assumes comparable extraction and recovery between bare and root-associated soil. Taxo-
nomic phylum membership supplies a compositional baseline; functional attribution requires the
corresponding pathway evidence.
    Missing XRF values and nonpositive concentrations receive a fixed replacement before log-
ratio transformation. Missing working-state guilds, phylum shares, pH and DNA values re-
ceive compartment medians, with global or fixed fallbacks when necessary. The raw columns
and observation flags distinguish these completed baselines from observations. The validation
panel inherits some completed covariates from this cube before its fold-specific imputation. Site-
compartment summaries and the sequence reference catalogues pool campaigns, including the
temporal holdout campaign. Consequently, the retrospective holdout evaluates a fixed reference
representation and pooled environmental context. A strictly prospective evaluation requires
freezing all preprocessing, covariate summaries and sequence references at the training cutoff.
    Ridge models assume an additive linear response on the specified transformed scale after
standardization. Spatial blocks group sites by longitude; they test within-transect transfer under
this block definition. Site bootstraps treat sites as the resampling units. Campaign holdouts
retain recurring sites. The selected penalties, target transforms, feature sets and partition rules
are fixed choices. Their uncertainty intervals describe resampling conditional on these choices.
The genus fits use a separate site-mean CLR panel and a separate browser representation; CLR
differences quantify changes relative to the geometric-mean reference composition.

Ontological and numerical assumptions
Subsumption monotonicity and partition sums require a nonnegative additive measure with a
shared denominator. Hydrogenase partitions refer to disjoint gene categories; an organism can
carry several categories, so summing carrier counts would require overlap accounting. The on-
tology module explicitly asserts the partitions and binds classes to numeric coordinates. Com-
pilation handles named-class subsumptions, disjoint unions and upper-bound annotations. The
full ontology’s arbitrary OWL entailments require a separate reasoning step.
    The constitutive preprocessing reserves at least 4% of the phylum simplex for other prokary-
otes, clips intact-cell DNA to [0.01, 0.99], and sets the relic fraction to its complement. The two-
pool representation treats these fractions operationally and combines several biological states.

Missing inputs can receive defaults. The OCS is the fraction of evaluated states satisfying every
compiled constraint. It concerns algebraic coherence; ATP feasibility belongs to the metabolic
model and predictive accuracy belongs to held-out evaluation. The scenario preprocessing al-
ready imposes most equalities before projection. The projection theorem concerns the optimal
projection of the supplied vector onto a closed convex set containing the truth. Solver fallback,
preprocessing changes and arbitrary weighted metrics require their own checks. The unit-weight
full-state objective combines fractions, GE and power on their stored numerical scales.

Mechanistic hypotheses and intervention versions
Every scenario holds the baseline and unmodified coefficients fixed while changing designated
inputs. This operational intervention assumes the response function remains valid under the new
exposure. Calibration from paired root and bulk samples assumes that the contrast transports
to a planted mosaic; matching that same calibration contrast measures implementation fidelity.
Transfer to inoculated shrubs additionally assumes establishment, expression and dose efficacy.
Species names and gene labels identify the scenario hypothesis, while the common response
equations determine its numerical outcome. The fixed phylogenetic attenuation dictionary is
unused in these evaluations; the evaluated scenarios include no fitted phylogenetic adjustment.
     Rainfall is a prescribed pulse entering a homogeneous 0.10 m column, with fixed porosity,
infiltration and drying coefficients. The response kernel, Shannon gain, heterotroph dilution,
DNA resuscitation and desiccation terms are prescribed. Day zero represents the pre-pulse
state; days 1–14 sample the response after infiltration. The instantaneous post-pulse maximum
moisture and minimum gas transport remain unresolved at this daily timestep. Cloud-seeding
efficacy, runoff, lateral flow and repeated-event hydrology require additional models. Diffusion is
a relative Millington–Quirk response multiplied by capacity and activity factors. The model uses
a fixed atmospheric reference and a capacity-to-power scaling; a spatial concentration-gradient
and gas-supply balance would supply additional physical constraints.
     Revegetation assumes an area mosaic with root and bare zones. Root depletion and
biomass multipliers derive from the pooled field contrast; inoculation gains, phylum shifts,
the canopy-diversity response and DNA viability factors are prescribed. Area-weighted GE,
biomass-weighted GE and the absolute gene-density multiplier are distinct outputs. The carbon
module further assumes a clipped biomass multiplier raised to 0.35. Its additional plasmid
allocation term overlaps the supplied plasmid multiplier, so the combined response is a specified
amplification assumption.
     The brine model assumes effective conductivity EC/(1 + 0.55q) for composition index q.
Logistic community responses, salinity thresholds, inoculation rescue and conductivity-dependent
water content and porosity are prescribed. The reference conductivities were measured in pool
water; predicting soil exposure assumes transport from discharge to porewater. Ion activity,
mineral saturation, water residence time, organism-specific tolerance and toxicity would require
additional measurements. In the brine power calculation water content changes from 0.045 to
0.085 at EC 50 mS cm−1 and porosity from 0.40 to 0.32 at EC 80 mS cm−1 .
     The refugium model assumes cooling of 11 ◦ C per intact-horizon fraction and 7 ◦ C per shade
fraction. Thermal loss begins above an effective 48 ◦ C, with separate prescribed effects on guilds,
Shannon diversity and the Bacillus survival index. Betaine changes both maintenance and the
thermal-response denominator. These dose-response curves assume transport from the motivat-
ing biological evidence to community-scale intervention effects.
     Grazing converts oryx, gazelle and camel densities to assumed disturbance units. A
rotational-management multiplier of 0.55 changes camel impact. Prescribed seed dispersal,
browsing, nutrient inputs, compatible-solute supply and compaction functions determine the
response. Even zero animal density retains baseline cover 0.08 and fixed moisture 0.020, so a
zero-input calculation is a model reference state. The ARG score and its 0.25 threshold are
assumed screening rules requiring endpoint validation before biosafety use.

    The seismic model prescribes compaction from line density, axle passes and grading, with
a nodal mitigation factor of 0.18. First-order hydrocarbon disappearance uses a baseline rate
0.0025 d−1 plus a dose- and porosity-dependent increment up to 0.0155 d−1 . Its vanadium
increment, pore clogging, toxicity losses and remediation diversity gain are assumed. Hydro-
carbon disappearance is the represented endpoint; product toxicity, mineralization and release
of contaminants require separate assays. Version-ambiguity intervals use fixed leaf sensitivities
for alternative realizations of a coarse intervention. They quantify allocation uncertainty within
that linear surrogate, conditional on its sensitivities and minimum shares.

Reduced metabolism, virome and uncertainty
The ten-flux LP uses prescribed energy yields, nitrogen demand, capacities, maintenance and
objective weights. It combines ammonia and nitrate in one external supply and uses a coarse
sulfur-redox term linked to available donors. Its fluxes have model units. Growth and carbon-
fixation outputs are allocation indices conditional on the resource constraints. The fallback
removes anabolic fluxes and excludes DOC from its catabolic maintenance allocation. Native
HiGHS status distinguishes proved infeasibility from other solver terminations, although the
current biological label is shared across unsuccessful terminations. The reported dark-proteome
fraction is a prescribed coding-composition response, and its legacy “active” output is an alias of
the same value.
    Virome and plasmid richness follow prescribed exponential and multiplicative response func-
tions. The coefficient 0.254 transfers a companion Mantel correlation as an assumed slope. Viral
and plasmid richness have floors of one and two; TPM, lysogeny and ARG outputs use fixed
response rules. The supplied climate, pH and evaporite variables have no numerical effect in
this implementation. A fitted joint species model and a calibrated ARG endpoint would require
separate estimation.
    Portfolio scores combine six simulations started independently from the same baseline. Their
means and weighted sums assume comparability across different endpoints and time horizons.
Joint feedback, shared resource depletion and interaction between modules require a coupled
state transition. P1 is a prescribed current-use portfolio with discharge, grazing and survey
activity; P0 is the separately calculated baseline. Utility depends on both the candidate set and
subjective weights. The 200 Monte Carlo draws perturb objective scores using fixed Gaussian
noise scales. Their quantiles and rank frequencies express sensitivity to that perturbation model.
Parameter distributions, structural alternatives and sampling uncertainty remain distinct sources
requiring propagation through the engines.

Regional carbon and browser interpretation
Carbon coefficients, clipping thresholds, reference uptake, annualization, retention fractions and
the regional background are specified in Methods. The low and high combinations are sensi-
tivity envelopes. The inferred shrub cover, NPP transfer, mineral-source fraction and retention
factors are assumptions. Long-term permanence, respiration, water delivery, establishment and
operating emissions require a stock-and-flow lifecycle model. Soil hydrogen uptake is reported
separately as a climate-equivalent contribution under an assumed marginal atmospheric response.
Regional policy calculations omit several levers exposed by the site models, including rainfall,
wild-ungulate density, refugium integrity and oil remediation.
    The 45 km corridor mask uses an approximate degree-distance conversion and interpolated
climate and elevation bounds. Interpolation inside observed ranges is a geometric rule, whose
relation to prediction error and intervention-dose support requires empirical evaluation. Nearest-
site transfer includes imputed states: 35 of the 128 supported cells inherit imputed surface guild
baselines. The P3/P4 assignment is a fixed rule. The constant cell area and regional mean
background assume representativeness beyond the directly sampled area.
