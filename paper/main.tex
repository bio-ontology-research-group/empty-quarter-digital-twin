\begin{abstract}
Managing drylands requires knowing how interventions on water, surface cover and soil change microbial processes, but genomic evidence, soil-process mechanisms and agronomic models describe different quantities. We built a digital twin of the Rub' al-Khali from 551 primary soil metagenomes that separates biological definitions, deduced from OWL axioms and genome evidence, from the causal mechanisms that interventions replace. We composed the twin with external agronomic, stoichiometric and microclimate models through typed, validity-checked interfaces and propagated joint parameter uncertainty through 14 management questions. Splitting rain into small events, shading, mulching, solar-panel cover and cloud seeding changed atmospheric hydrogen uptake in a consistent direction in at least 95\% of parameter draws. The direction of the response to warming, biochar, compaction and irrigation interval depended on soil water retention and temperature sensitivity. Deduction gave exact answers: communities without mapped CO-oxidizing genomes had zero CO response under every intervention.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Land management in hot deserts increasingly includes irrigation with brackish water, solar parks, surface covers and soil amendments. Where vegetation is sparse, microorganisms that oxidize atmospheric hydrogen and carbon monoxide sustain much of the biological activity in the soil, and some of them couple this energy to carbon fixation \cite{ji2017atmospheric,leung2020energetic,jordaan2020gobi}. Soil microorganisms are also the largest sink of atmospheric hydrogen \cite{ehhalt2009h2cycle}, a sink whose capacity matters as hydrogen use expands \cite{ocko2022hydrogen,warwick2023atmospheric}. Predicting how a management action changes these processes requires three kinds of knowledge: which organisms carry the relevant capacities, how water, salt, temperature and pore structure control their activity, and how the action changes those physical conditions. The Rub' al-Khali, the world's largest continuous sand desert \cite{nasaRubAlKhali}, is a setting in which all three must be connected from little prior data.

Each kind of knowledge has its own models. Metabolic annotation tools such as DRAM and METABOLIC organize genome evidence into pathways and potential biogeochemical roles \cite{shaffer2020dram,zhou2022metabolic}; they describe capacity independently of environmental conditions. Soil process models connect moisture-dependent biological activation with gas transport \cite{bertagni2021moisture}, but they represent the community by rate parameters. Agronomic models translate irrigation water salinity and crop water demand into root-zone salinity and yield \cite{ayers1985fao29,allen1998fao56}, without microbial processes. Digital-twin programmes for drylands integrate Earth observation, vegetation models and machine-learning emulation \cite{twindrylands}. Answering a management question for a desert soil requires a combination of these models. Combining them by shared variables leaves three questions open: which quantities an intervention can change and which are fixed by definition, whether each model is used within the domain where it is valid, and whether the combined uncertainty still determines the direction of the answer.

Ontological deduction and causal inference address the first of these questions. Ontological axioms determine the consequences of biological definitions, including pathway conjunctions, alternative reactions and the meaning of a measured quantity \cite{owl2direct2012}. Causal mechanisms determine how physical states change when specified inputs are manipulated \cite{pearl2009causality}. In a structural causal model, an intervention replaces mechanisms, whereas definitions and other non-causal constraints hold in every regime \cite{bochman2015pearls}. Separating the two zones determines which quantities can be manipulated, so that water storage can be set but uptake or a pathway label cannot. It makes deduced quantities identical across regimes, which yields zero effects that follow from the evidence without simulation. It also identifies interventions on defined quantities, such as total water potential, and broad treatments, such as a fixed amount of wetting, that remain ambiguous until a version is specified \cite{spirtes2004ambiguous,vanderweele2013multiple}.

Here we construct EQ-BioDT, a causal--ontological digital twin of the Rub' al-Khali built from primary genome-resolved observations, and use it to answer management questions. We extend the stratification of definitions and mechanisms to composition with external models: every connection between models is typed by an ontology term and a unit, every model declares the domain in which it is valid and refuses inputs outside it, and deductive gates carry exact zeros across models. We propagate joint parameter uncertainty through each composed question and report, for every intervention, the share of parameter draws in which the effect has each direction. We test the twin's moisture-response mechanism against independent measurements from the Namib and Sonoran deserts and from temperate soils, and a composed salinity chain against a Saudi alfalfa field trial. Data, ontology, model code and reproducible analyses are available in the public repository.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Primary observations and ontological deduction define the biological state of the twin}
The twin represents 551 soil metagenomes collected during five campaigns between March 2023 and October 2025. They cover 217 site--campaign visits at 59 of the 60 core sites of a repeated Rub' al-Khali transect and two auxiliary locations (Fig.~\ref{fig:primary}a,b). Surface sand, subsurface sand and root-adjacent soil contribute 117, 166 and 268 metagenomes, respectively. We mapped each metagenome against a catalogue of \ReferenceMAGs{} metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs), and \MappedSamples{} metagenomes have usable mapped compositions. These compositions are the evidence from which the twin deduces which capacities a community carries (Fig.~\ref{fig:primary}c).

We encoded each MAG as an OWL class defined by its observed KEGG orthologs and marker genes and classified the MAGs with an OWL reasoner (Methods). Applying the complete KEGG M00165 definition supports \CBBFull{} Calvin--Benson--Bassham (CBB) candidate MAGs, of which \HtwoCBB{} also carry hydrogenase markers and \DualHtwoCO{} additionally carry a form-I CO-dehydrogenase marker. The definition matters: a screen for Rubisco alone admits 1,078 MAGs. Genome linkage also determines a quantity that community-level annotation leaves open. From hydrogenase and CBB carrier shares alone, the share of the community carrying both pathways lies in an interval, and this interval has positive width in \AmbiguousSamples{} of \MappedSamples{} mapped samples (median width 6.13 percentage points). Genome-resolved conjunction determines a specific value within it (Fig.~\ref{fig:deduction}a).

The deduced classes decide which reactions a calculation may admit. In a conditional resource query, a hydrogenase-plus-CBB candidate without CO-carrier entailment has a carbohydrate-carbon ceiling of 0.0674~nmol~m$^{-2}$~s$^{-1}$; admitting CO oxidation without its supporting annotation raises the ceiling 7.42-fold, to 0.500 (Fig.~\ref{fig:deduction}b). An independent parser of the KEGG module expression reproduces every classification, and a flat implementation that admits reactions from the same tabular evidence gives the same bound. The ontology therefore contributes an explicit, checkable derivation of eligibility from shared premises. The deduced carriers also differ between compartments: subsurface sand exceeds root-adjacent soil in mapped CBB carrier share by 4.38 percentage points across 107 matched site--campaign groups (site-bootstrap 95\% interval 2.91--6.00; Suppl. Table~\ref{S-tab:contrasts}).

\subsection{Interventions replace mechanisms while deduced quantities stay fixed}
The twin represents definitions and mechanisms in one stratified graph (Fig.~\ref{fig:graph}). Ontological axioms and evidence determine memberships and carrier shares; constitutive maps define water content, water potential, gas diffusivity and chemical power; structural mechanisms propagate water, salt, activation, kinetic coefficients and uptake through time. An intervention replaces mechanisms or input values and leaves every definition unchanged. Of the \GraphNodes{} represented quantities, \AdmissibleNodes{} can be intervened on: \AdmissibleInputs{} exogenous inputs, such as water addition, salt in added water and temperature, and \AdmissibleStates{} states with a physical handle, the water and salt inventories. The twin rejects requests to clamp uptake, a kinetic coefficient, a carrier share or a pathway membership and names the zone that makes them inadmissible.

The graph also identifies interventions that remain ambiguous until a version is specified. Setting water content translates uniquely to water storage, but setting total water potential does not, because draining water and adding salt both lower the potential. After a 12~mm pulse, both versions of $\mathrm{do}(\psi=-0.4~\mathrm{MPa})$ give the same activation, which depends on potential alone. Three-day hydrogen uptake, however, is \PsiMildUptakeMatric{} for the matric and \PsiMildUptakeOsmotic{}~$\mu$mol~m$^{-2}$ for the osmotic version, because water content also controls gas diffusivity. The twin detects this ambiguity before any simulation, from the path through water content and diffusivity that bypasses water potential, and requires a version. A management action such as adding 12~mm of water is ambiguous in the same way: across \VersionMembers{} parameter combinations, a single pulse exceeded four pulses in \VersionSingleFirst{} and four pulses exceeded a single pulse in \VersionFourFirst{} (Suppl. Fig.~\ref{S-fig:scenarios}). Every management question below therefore specifies its schedule.

Deduced quantities have no causal ancestors and are therefore identical in every regime. \ClosedCOGates{} of \MappedSamples{} sampled communities contain no mapped CO-carrier genome. Under the deductive gate, their CO uptake is zero in every regime, so every intervention has zero effect on it; the twin confirmed this in all \ClosedCOZeroEffects{} simulated contrasts. The result also follows without simulation, as a derivation in the $\mathrm{do}$-calculus. Ordinary $d$-separation cannot remove a water addition from CO uptake, because water content reaches CO uptake through gas diffusivity. Conditioning on the deductive closure of a zero CO-carrier share fixes the kinetic coefficient and the uptake, and licenses the deletion of the intervention by Rule~3. Every sampled community contains mapped hydrogenase carriers, so the gate leaves hydrogen uptake unchanged. For field observations with a latent season, the effect of water storage on hydrogen uptake is identified by adjustment for the recorded water inputs, the previous water state, porosity and either temperature or evaporation demand; no adjustment set exists when neither of the last two is measured (Suppl. Table~\ref{S-tab:identification}).

\subsection{Independent desert measurements confirm the direction of the moisture response and require local parameters for its magnitude}
Most management answers depend on the twin's moisture-response mechanism, in which biological activation rises as water becomes available while gas transport falls as water fills the pores. We tested this mechanism against independent hydration and field measurements from two deserts, preserving each study's measurement units and experimental support (Fig.~\ref{fig:external}). In Namib Desert microcosms, added water increased atmospheric hydrogen oxidation in the two fog-zone soils with quantified dry and wet rates, with mean wet-to-dry ratios of 41.3 at C14-2 and 19.4 at C14-4 \cite{tribbia2026namib}. The twin predicts this positive direction at bulk densities of 1.2 and 1.5~g~cm$^{-3}$. At 1.8~g~cm$^{-3}$, hydrated C14-2 soil nearly fills the pore space, gas diffusion collapses and the predicted direction reverses. The twin also predicts zero dry uptake at both active sites, which the measurements contradict.

In a Sonoran Desert incubation, uptake increased from 0.10 to 0.20~g~g$^{-1}$ gravimetric water content in every plot, by 0.32--0.51~nmol~m$^{-2}$~s$^{-1}$ \cite{buzzard2022h2uptake}, whereas the twin with reference retention parameters predicts a decline over this interval. We then evaluated quantitative transfer against 64 Sonoran field measurements, aggregated into 16 plot--campaign means, holding out each entire campaign in turn. The frozen reference model had RMSE 1.65~nmol~m$^{-2}$~s$^{-1}$, and refitting only its kinetic coefficient gave 1.72. Calibrating the soil-response parameters within a fixed grid reduced the error to 0.69, compared with 0.79 for the training mean and 1.35 for a linear moisture--temperature model (Table~\ref{tab:transfer}). Between three temperate soils, a common activation--diffusion amplitude fitted on two soils improved held-soil prediction for the forest and sand soils and performed worse for the meadow soil \cite{reji2025h2sink}.

The mechanism therefore transfers in direction across deserts, while its magnitude, and near saturation its direction, depend on retention, porosity and temperature-response parameters that are unmeasured in Rub' al-Khali sands. We treat these parameters as uncertain in every management question.

\subsection{Composition turns management actions into twin interventions}
Most management questions begin and end outside the soil-process core. A farmer specifies irrigation by water salinity and schedule and judges it by crop yield, whereas the twin takes daily water and NaCl inputs and computes gas uptake. We therefore composed the twin with external models that translate a management action into twin interventions and a twin trajectory into a management endpoint (Fig.~\ref{fig:composition}a). Each external model has typed input and output ports, each port names an ontology term and a unit, and two models can be connected only when term and unit agree. Each port also declares the interval within which its model is valid. An input outside that interval returns a refusal that names the port, the value and the interval. A downstream model may declare a deductive gate, so that a community without the required capacity receives an exact zero together with its ontological justification. Every output carries its provenance: the twin, an external model or deduction. The 42 constants of the external models are recorded with their sources; we verified 23 against the cited source, 10 are modeller assumptions and 9 could not be checked (Suppl. Table~\ref{S-tab:constants}).

We tested a composed chain against a field trial at Hada Al-Sham, Saudi Arabia, in which alfalfa was irrigated at 3.12 and 7.81~dS~m$^{-1}$ with and without the inoculant \textit{Enterobacter} sp. SA187 \cite{dezelicourt2018sa187}. Without the inoculant, the high-salinity treatment yielded 0.80 of the low-salinity yield for fresh biomass and 0.83 for dry biomass. The standard fixed leaching factor, $\mathrm{EC_e}=1.5\,\mathrm{EC_w}$ \cite{ayers1985fao29}, combined with the Maas--Hoffman alfalfa response \cite{allen1998fao56}, predicts a ratio of 0.36 (Fig.~\ref{fig:composition}b). The twin's bare-sand salt balance predicts 0.883, because drainage removes salt without a crop-transpiration sink. Fitting the leaching factor to the observed ratio gives $f=0.61$ for fresh and $f=0.56$ for dry biomass. These values imply a root-zone $\mathrm{EC_e}$ of 1.7 to 1.9~dS~m$^{-1}$ under the low-salinity treatment, close to the measured site soil conductivity of 1.95~dS~m$^{-1}$, a quantity independent of the fit (Fig.~\ref{fig:composition}c). The standard rule implies 4.68~dS~m$^{-1}$.

The fitted chain then answers the question the trial was designed for. With the fitted fresh-biomass leaching factor and the SA187 yield multiplier, the predicted inoculated relative yield at 7.81~dS~m$^{-1}$ has a median of \QAlfalfaSaMedian{} (joint 5--95\% interval \QAlfalfaSaLow{}--\QAlfalfaSaHigh{}). Constants that we verified in the trial publication carry almost all of its uncertainty, with a group total-order index of \QAlfalfaVerifiedGroupSTPct\%. A single trial at two salinities identifies the inoculant benefit as a constant multiplier across the tested range of 3.12 to 7.81~dS~m$^{-1}$.

\subsection{Joint uncertainty separates determined management answers from soil-dependent ones}
We posed 14 questions about desert land management and microbial communities (Table~\ref{tab:answers}). They ask how the timing, amount and salinity of water, surface covers, warming, soil disturbance and amendments change hydrogen uptake; how community composition and inoculation change CO uptake; how much carbon hydrogen-oxidizing autotrophs can fix; and how an inoculant changes crop yield under saline irrigation. Each question is a composed chain evaluated on a common 14-day baseline of a single 12~mm rain event. We sampled the 13 twin process parameters and the external constants of each chain jointly, evaluating \UQLevels{} scenario levels with \UQEvaluations{} model runs (Methods). For each intervention we report the median change, its 5--95\% interval and the share of parameter draws in which the change has each sign.

Absolute uptake is poorly constrained. Fourteen-day hydrogen uptake on the baseline has a median of \QBaseHtwoMedian{}~$\mu$mol~m$^{-2}$ and a 5--95\% interval of \QBaseHtwoLow{} to \QBaseHtwoHigh{}~$\mu$mol~m$^{-2}$, and the H$_2$ rate constant explains most of this variance (total-order Sobol' index $S_T=\QBaseHtwoSTkHtwo$). The rate constant scales intervened and baseline runs alike. The paired percentage change between them therefore cancels it and answers a management question more sharply than either absolute value.

Seven interventions changed hydrogen uptake in the same direction in at least 95\% of parameter draws (Fig.~\ref{fig:answers}a). Partitioning 12~mm of rain into four 3~mm events reduced uptake in \QRainFourPneg\% of draws, by a median of \QRainFourMedian\%, and daily drizzle of the same total reduced it in \QRainDrizzlePneg\% of draws. Each small addition is largely removed by evaporation before the soil reaches the water potential at which hydrogen oxidizers become active. Full organic mulch increased uptake in \QMulchFullPpos\% of draws (median \QMulchFullMedian\%), and full solar-panel cover in \QSolarFullPpos\% (median \QSolarFullMedian\%), because both reduce evaporation and prolong the active period. Cloud seeding that adds 10\% to each rain event increased uptake in every draw (median \QCloudTenMedian\%). A full shade net reduced uptake in every draw (median \QShadeFullMedian\%) because it cools the soil, and warming the soil by 4~$^\circ$C at fixed moisture increased uptake in every draw (median \QHotterSoilFourMedian\%).

The direction of the remaining interventions depends on the parameters. Climate warming of 4~$^\circ$C with unchanged rainfall reduced uptake in \QHotterFourPneg\% of draws (median \QHotterFourMedian\%, 5--95\% interval \QHotterFourLow{} to \QHotterFourHigh\%). Warming accelerates the reaction but also raises the evaporative demand, which shortens the period of activity. Biochar at 5\% w/w reduced uptake in \QBiocharFivePneg\% of draws and 50 off-road vehicle passes in \QCompactionFiftyPneg\%. Daily irrigation at a seasonal demand of 6~mm~day$^{-1}$ decreased uptake in \QDripDailyPneg\% of draws (median change \QDripDailyMedian\%), and irrigation every ten days in \QDripTenDayPneg\%. Sustained irrigation keeps the soil wet enough to restrict gas diffusion, while widely spaced pulses restore dry intervals. Irrigating daily with brackish water at 8~dS~m$^{-1}$ changed uptake by a median of \QSaltyEightMedian\%, compared with \QSaltyFreshDailyMedian\% for the same irrigation at 0.5~dS~m$^{-1}$; the added water determines most of the response.

\subsection{Soil water retention and temperature sensitivity decide the undetermined answers}
Variance decomposition identifies the measurements that would determine the undetermined answers (Fig.~\ref{fig:answers}c). The retention exponent $b$ and the air-entry potential $\psi_e$ of the soil water-retention curve explained most of the variance in the percentage changes for rain timing, irrigation, salinity, mulch and biochar; for rain splitting, $b$ alone reached $S_T=\QRainFourSTOne$. The two parameters set how long the soil stays above the activation threshold after a water addition, which is the quantity most interventions act on. The temperature coefficient $Q_{10}$ dominated the response to climate warming ($S_T=\QHotterFourSTOne$). The H$_2$ rate constant and $b$ dominated the response to compaction ($S_T=\QCompactionFiftySTOne$ and $\QCompactionFiftySTTwo$), because compaction reduces the gas-filled pore space while it raises the water saturation reached after rain.

These dominant inputs are assumptions of the model. In \UQHeadlinesAssumGeEightyFive{} of the 14 headline outputs, the inputs that we classified as modeller assumptions had a group total-order index of at least 0.85. The exception is the inoculated alfalfa yield, whose variance came from constants verified in the trial publication. Measured retention curves and temperature responses of hydrogen uptake in Rub' al-Khali sands are therefore the experiments that would determine most of the undetermined answers.

\subsection{Deduction answers community and carbon questions exactly}
Questions about the microbial community are answered in part by deduction. Among the samples evaluated for the CO question, \QMissingCOClosedSamples{} contain no mapped CO-carrier genome. Their CO uptake is exactly zero in every parameter draw and under every intervention, because the deductive gate fixes it before any mechanism is evaluated. In each of the \QMissingCOOpenSamples{} samples with mapped carriers, 14-day CO uptake has a median of \QMissingCOOpenMedian{}~$\mu$mol~m$^{-2}$ (5--95\% interval \QMissingCOOpenLow{}--\QMissingCOOpenHigh{} in sample V53PRr2), with the CO rate constant explaining most of the variance ($S_T=\QMissingCOOpenSTOne$).

Inoculation is a constitutive intervention: it recomputes the deduced carrier shares of a counterfactual community from the existing memberships and leaves the ontology and genome evidence unchanged (Methods). Introducing a CO-carrier MAG into a community whose CO gate is closed opens the gate, whereas introducing a non-carrier MAG keeps CO uptake at zero. In a community whose gate is already open, the response depends on how carrier share couples to the rate coefficient. Under the deductive gate, a 1\% inoculum has no effect; under an assumed proportionality between carrier share and rate coefficient, it increases CO uptake by \QAddingOnePctMedian\% in a sample with a 47.8\% initial carrier share. The same assumption separates compartments: under proportionality, the single-pulse effect on hydrogen uptake in subsurface sand exceeds that in root-adjacent soil by \ContrastDeepRootEst{}~$\mu$mol~m$^{-2}$ (site-bootstrap 95\% interval \ContrastDeepRootLow{}--\ContrastDeepRootHigh{}; \ContrastDeepRootVisits{} visits), and under the gate the difference vanishes (Suppl. Fig.~\ref{S-fig:scenarios}). Uptake measurements resolved by carrier share would test the coupling.

Chaining twin hydrogen uptake to the knallgas CBB stoichiometry of 1/6~mol~CO$_2$ per mol~H$_2$ \cite{bongers1970energy} bounds the carbon that hydrogen-oxidizing autotrophs can fix. In the sample with the highest autotroph share (2.64\%), the annualized bound has a median of \QCarbonBoundMedian{}~g~C~m$^{-2}$~yr$^{-1}$ (5--95\% interval \QCarbonBoundLow{}--\QCarbonBoundHigh{}), \QCarbonBoundPctTen\% of a 10~g~C~m$^{-2}$~yr$^{-1}$ dryland biocrust reference flux \cite{elbert2012cryptogamic}. In samples without entailed autotrophic candidates, the bound is zero by deduction while hydrogen oxidation continues. Doubling atmospheric hydrogen above the 0.53~ppm background \cite{novelli1999h2} doubled hydrogen uptake in every draw, because uptake is first order in the surface concentration and oxygen supply remained unlimiting. Hydrogen-supported carbon fixation in these sands is therefore a small carbon flux compared with that of biocrusts.

\section{Discussion}
EQ-BioDT answers management questions for a sand desert by composing a causal--ontological twin with external models and propagating joint uncertainty through each composition. Its answers fall into three classes. For rain splitting, shading, mulching, solar-panel cover and cloud seeding, the direction of the change in hydrogen uptake holds in at least 95\% of parameter draws; these answers can inform management before the magnitudes are calibrated. For climate warming, biochar, compaction and irrigation interval, the direction depends on the soil water-retention curve and the temperature coefficient, and the twin names these as the measurements that would decide it. For community questions, deduction gives exact zeros that require neither simulation nor parameter values.

The method connects models that have been used separately. Soil process models predict the moisture response of trace-gas uptake for given parameters \cite{bertagni2021moisture}, agronomic models predict root-zone salinity and yield from irrigation \cite{ayers1985fao29,allen1998fao56}, and dryland digital twins integrate Earth observation with vegetation models \cite{twindrylands}. Composition through typed ports adds three properties to such a coupling. Each connection is checked for meaning and unit, each model is used only within its declared domain, and each output states whether the twin, an external model or a deduction produced it. The Hada Al-Sham comparison shows why the domain matters: the twin's bare-sand salt balance, used for an irrigated crop, overpredicts the yield ratio, and the standard leaching rule underpredicts it, while a leaching factor fitted to yield implies a root-zone conductivity close to the measured soil conductivity.

The ontology contributes definitions and derivations. With the same premises, the flat implementation gives the same predictions, and the ontology adds the separation of invariant definitions from severable mechanisms, the deductive zero effects and the declared measurement correspondences. This separation also localizes failures. A definition cannot be refuted by an uptake measurement, so the failed Namib dry-activity prediction implicates the activation threshold and the retention relation, and a measured CO uptake in a community without mapped CO carriers would implicate incomplete genome evidence.

The determined answers have direct management readings for bare sand. If the soil hydrogen sink and the microbial activity that depends on it are to be maintained, water should arrive in large, widely spaced events, and covers that reduce evaporation help while shading that only cools the soil reduces uptake. Brackish irrigation at 8~dS~m$^{-1}$ acts on microbial uptake mainly through its water, so the choice of irrigation schedule matters more than the salinity of the water. The upper bound on hydrogen-supported carbon fixation lies two orders of magnitude below a biocrust reference flux, so carbon management in these sands depends on other fluxes.

Several limits define the scope of these answers. The uncertainty intervals cover parameters and constants at fixed model structure; they exclude structural errors in the process equations and the external models. The twin represents bare sand without a crop-transpiration sink, so root-zone salinity under vegetation requires an external leaching model, and crop-yield and carbon endpoints come from external models driven by twin states. Of the 42 external constants, 19 are modeller assumptions or values whose source could not be checked. The proportional coupling between carrier share and rate coefficient is untested. The external evaluation was retrospective, the Sonoran calibration selected boundary values of its parameter grid, and the composed salinity chain was tested against one field trial at two salinities. The represented geographic extent follows the primary sampling footprint, and pathway support is limited by assembly completeness and enzyme specificity.

These limits point to specific experiments. Controlled wetting of Rub' al-Khali sands with measured retention curves, temperature responses and simultaneous hydrogen uptake would determine most of the undetermined answers. Field manipulations of rain timing and surface cover would test the determined ones. Uptake measurements resolved by carrier share would test the coupling between community composition and rate, and genome-resolved transcript, protein and isotope observations would test how the deduced capacities are expressed. Forecasts frozen before further sampling would add prospective evidence.

\section{Methods}
\subsection{A stratified causal--ontological model}
The twin separates three kinds of change. An observation update changes the evidence $E$ (annotations, marker assignments, mapping weights). A model revision changes the ontology $K$ or a mechanism. An intervention changes exogenous physical inputs while $E$ and $K$ remain fixed. Selecting a sampled community conditions the twin on evidence and is not an intervention.

Following the treatment of non-causal constraints in structural causal models \cite{bochman2015pearls,pearl2009causality}, the model has two zones. The invariant zone contains the OWL axioms $K$, the deduced memberships $D_K(E)$ and constitutive maps $\Phi$: definitions such as $\theta=W/(1000L)$, $W_{\max}=1000L\phi$, the retention and osmotic relations, total potential, Millington--Quirk diffusivity, carrier shares, chemical power and the declared observation maps. The causal zone contains structural mechanisms for water storage $W_t$, salt inventory $N_t$, activation $A_t$, the kinetic coefficient $k_{g,t}$ and uptake $J_{g,t}$. Exogenous inputs are daily water addition, NaCl in added water, evaporation demand, temperature and soil parameters. The causal graph has an edge from each argument of a mechanism or constitutive map to its output and is acyclic when unrolled over time (Fig.~\ref{fig:graph}); the executable graph records the zone, unit, equation and manipulability of every node.

Deduction enters the mechanisms through a community factor $\gamma_g$ for gas $g$. For a sampled community $s$, $q_s(C)$ is the mapped-read share of MAGs that the reasoner entails to belong to class $C$, and
\begin{equation}
k_{g,t}=k_{g,\mathrm{ref}}\,\gamma_g\,A(\psi_t)\,Q_{10}^{(T_t-T_{\mathrm{ref}})/10},\qquad
\gamma_g\in\Bigl\{1,\;\mathbf{1}[q_s(C_g)>0],\;\frac{q_s(C_g)}{\operatorname{median}_{s'}q_{s'}(C_g)}\Bigr\},
\end{equation}
with $C_{\mathrm{H_2}}$ the hydrogenase-carrier class and $C_{\mathrm{CO}}$ the form-I CO-dehydrogenase carrier class. The three options are the reference community, a deductive gate and an explicitly assumed proportionality between carrier share and rate coefficient. The reference scenarios use $\gamma_g=1$. The same memberships admit reactions to the resource ceiling.

An intervention $\mathrm{do}(X_I=x_I)$ replaces the mechanisms or input values of the targets $I$ and leaves every other mechanism, every constitutive map, $K$, $E$ and the initial state unchanged. It is admissible when every target is an exogenous input or a causal state with a physical handle (water or salt inventory at the start of a day) and some state satisfies all constitutive maps and unsevered mechanisms; for example, a reduced porosity that makes initial water exceed pore capacity is rejected. Deduced memberships, carrier shares, constituted quantities and the outcome mechanisms $A$, $k$ and $J$ cannot be clamped. A target in the invariant zone is defined through its constituents. Setting water content, $\mathrm{do}(\theta=\theta^*)$, translates uniquely to $\mathrm{do}(W=1000L\theta^*)$. Setting total water potential is an ambiguous manipulation \cite{spirtes2004ambiguous}: matric and osmotic versions reach the same $\psi$ by different water and salt inventories. Its effect on activation is version-independent because $A$ depends only on $\psi$, whereas its effect on uptake depends on the version through the path $W\to\theta\to D_g\to J_g$ that bypasses $\psi$. The implementation derives such version relevance from the graph and requires a version for ambiguous targets. Likewise, a fixed total water addition over a horizon is realized by several schedules, and its effect is defined only for a stated schedule or mixture of schedules \cite{vanderweele2013multiple}.

Three consequences follow from the construction and are checked at run time. First, $D_K(E)$, $q_s$ and $\gamma_g$ have no causal ancestors, so they are identical in every admissible regime, and every entailed subsumption lifts to the shares; for example, the share of hydrogenase-plus-CBB candidates never exceeds the hydrogenase-carrier share. Second, constitutive identities hold along every intervened trajectory. Third, if $\gamma_g=0$ for a community, uptake of gas $g$ is zero in every regime, so every interventional contrast on that uptake is zero by deduction. Absence of entailment reflects the current genome evidence, so a measured uptake in such a community would show that the evidence missed a carrier. Paired regimes share the initial state, non-targeted inputs, parameters and evidence, so their difference is a unit-level counterfactual contrast within the deterministic model.

Deduction also enters identification. For an observed stratum $\mathbf{Z}=\mathbf{z}$, the deductive value closure $\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbf{Z}=\mathbf{z})$ contains every quantity whose value is entailed by the definitions together with the mechanisms of quantities that no admissible intervention can replace; because these mechanisms are never severed, the entailed values hold in every regime. The closure determines a constitutive quantity when all its arguments are known, and propagates zeros: $q_s(C_g)=0$ entails $\gamma_g=0$, $k_g=0$ and $J_g=0$, and $\psi\leq\psi_d$ entails $A=0$. Following the context-specific extension of $d$-separation to deterministic and ontologically determined nodes \cite{geiger1990identifying}, the rules of the $\mathrm{do}$-calculus are evaluated on the time-unrolled graph with the conditioning set $\mathbf{Z}\cup\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbf{Z}=\mathbf{z})$, after removing edges whose mechanism is inert in the stratum, and an outcome fixed by the closure is treated as a constant. Back-door adjustment sets for field data are searched over observed non-descendants of the treatment in a graph with a latent season that drives water input, temperature and evaporation (Suppl. Section~\ref{S-sec:invariance}).

The interventional quantities are derived from the stated mechanisms, and compartment contrasts are associational. The external hydration microcosms set water content experimentally and therefore test interventional predictions, whereas field campaigns vary moisture and temperature jointly with season and test the mechanism observationally. Transfer between deserts is a transportability problem in which soil parameters and activation thresholds may differ between environments \cite{pearl2014external}; calibration re-estimates exactly these mechanisms. Because $K$ and $\Phi$ are definitional, a failed process prediction implicates a mechanism, an assumed parameter or an observation map.

For carrier sets $A$ and $B$ measured on the same reference universe, their marginal shares imply
\begin{equation}
\max\{0,q_i(A)+q_i(B)-1\}\leq q_i(A\cap B)\leq\min\{q_i(A),q_i(B)\}.
\end{equation}
Genome-resolved annotations determine the intersection. Observation maps preserve the endpoint, unit, denominator and experimental support of each quantity. The ontology declares measurement endpoints as disjoint classes and records which conversions are defined: a first-order coefficient converts to bottle uptake given headspace gas amount and soil mass, gravimetric to volumetric water content requires bulk density, and no direct map relates a bottle rate to an areal flux without geometric and transport assumptions.

\subsection{Evidence inputs and module semantics}
The primary programme sampled surface soil, bulk subsurface soil at 5--10~cm and root-adjacent soil at the same depth. Soil was frozen in the field and stored for molecular analysis. The March 2023 field ledger records site coordinates, collection dates, biome and local features; the 59 core-site reference coordinates in this cohort match that ledger. The wider survey, including amplicon data, covers all 60 core sites; no metagenome is available from site 56. Metagenomic assemblies were binned, refined and dereplicated into species representatives, and genes on contigs removed during refinement were excluded from the representative annotations. The twin's molecular analysis starts from 551 metagenomes, \ReferenceMAGs{} reference MAGs, corrected post-refinement KO annotations, MAG quality and GTDB taxonomy, trace-gas marker assignments, CoverM mapping percentages, read-screen genome equivalents and curated collection dates. The versioned input snapshot includes each of these analysis inputs and checksums. Sequencing, assembly and primary annotation precede this analysis boundary. Genome equivalents use the screened marker-gene read counts and single-copy-gene normalizer, with a 60\% identity floor. They denote relative gene-copy capacity.

For mapped-read weight $w_{ig}$ of MAG $g$ in sample $i$, the share of a carrier set $A$ within reference set $G$ is
\begin{equation}
q_i(A)=\frac{\sum_{g\in A}w_{ig}}{\sum_{g\in G}w_{ig}}.
\end{equation}
This share is unavailable when the denominator is zero or any required mapping weight is nonfinite. The evidence builder checks unique sample--MAG pairs, nonnegative weights, reference membership and closure of mapped plus unmapped percentages to $100\pm0.02\%$. Mapped-MAG Shannon entropy uses the same normalized weights. Genome equivalents, read shares, DNA recovery, membrane integrity, biomass and activity are separate endpoints \cite{carini2016relicdna,lennon2018relicdna}.

The pathway rule implements the 11 conjunctive requirements of KEGG M00165 as retrieved on 28 October 2026. Alternatives within a requirement are disjunctive; K01602 is optional in the module definition. The regeneration branch accepts K01100, K11532 or K01086 for the bisphosphatase route, or K00616 or K13810 for the transaldolase route; 149 supported genomes use the transaldolase route without an annotated sedoheptulose bisphosphatase, a variant with experimental precedent \cite{frolov2019transaldolase}. The grouping expresses alternative support for the module requirement; the two routes retain their distinct biochemical meanings. A separate parser evaluates the original KEGG expression, including parentheses, alternative branches, complexes and optional components, against the full source KO table without importing the production step dictionary.

We encode each MAG as an OWL class with existential restrictions for its observed KOs and marker guilds. The CBB-support class is the intersection of the requirement classes. Hydrogenase and CO carrier classes derive from the respective marker hierarchies; their intersections with CBB support define candidate eligibility. The declared quality subset requires completeness at least 90\% and contamination below 5\%, a subset of the MIMAG high-quality criteria; \HtwoCBBStrict{} of the hydrogenase-plus-CBB candidates meet it. Domain assertions and a coarse four-bucket phylum partition support the exported ontology; detailed GTDB taxonomic summaries are kept as tabular annotations outside the ontology.

Each MAG class is a subclass of $\exists\,\mathsf{hasKO}.K$ for every KEGG orthology group $K$ to which at least one of its genes is assigned. Pathway, enzyme, chemical and environment classes are linked to the Gene Ontology, ChEBI, ENVO, PATO and the Units of Measurement Ontology by annotation mappings, because a genome class denotes the genome of an organism whereas these terms denote processes, functions, chemicals and environments; every mapped term was checked against the Ontology Lookup Service on 30 October 2026. Measurement endpoints are pairwise disjoint subclasses of the SIO measurement-value class, and declared observation-map classes record the assumptions each conversion requires. Kobayashi-MaRust v1.5.3 classifies the OWL ontology and produces verified subset-minimal justifications for every hydrogenase-plus-CBB candidate and every CO-carrier MAG, and for the disjointness of every pair of measurement endpoints. The exported membership artifact records the ontology, evidence, rules and reasoner identities. Before memberships are used, the runtime verifies the artifact checksums, the rule and evidence digests, the digest of the shipped ontology and the pinned reasoner digest. Missing entailment remains an admission decision under incomplete annotations. Numerical aggregation and conservation checks are implemented separately from OWL subsumption.

\subsection{Conditional regimes, water and salt balance}
The state $(W_t,N_t)$ comprises water storage in mm and NaCl inventory in mol~m$^{-2}$; daily forcing and soil parameters are the exogenous inputs of the preceding section.

For layer depth $L$ and porosity $\phi$, pore capacity is $W_{\max}=1000L\phi$~mm. At the start of a day, water $I$ with NaCl concentration $c_I$ adds $I$ to water and $Ic_I$ to salt; overflow removes a proportionate amount of the mixed salt. The day is divided into $n=96$ substeps. Each substep drains
\begin{equation}
D=\left[1-(1-d)^{1/n}\right]\max(0,W-fW_{\max}),
\end{equation}
where $d$ is the daily drainage fraction and $f$ the field-capacity fraction. Drainage removes $ND/W$ salt when $W>0$, followed by evaporation $\min(\mathrm{Ev}/n,W-D)$ without salt removal. Gas rates are evaluated at the midpoint of the pre- and post-substep water and salt inventories and integrated over $86400/n$ seconds. Increasing the resolution from 96 to 384 substeps per day changes reference-schedule uptake by at most 0.026\%. The complete defaults and assumptions are generated from the executable contract in Suppl. Sections~S1--S3.

Volumetric water content is $\theta=W/(1000L)$. Campbell retention gives matric potential $\psi_m=\psi_e(\theta/\phi)^{-b}$ for entry potential $\psi_e<0$ and exponent $b>0$ \cite{campbell1974retention}. In dilute NaCl, osmotic potential is $\psi_o=-2\varphi cRT\times10^{-3}$~MPa, where $c$ is mol~L$^{-1}$, $\varphi$ the osmotic coefficient, $R$ the gas constant and $T$ absolute temperature. Substeps outside the specified dilute domain, default 0.1~mol~L$^{-1}$, or with salt in dry soil make daily and integrated gas outputs unavailable. For total potential $\psi=\psi_m+\psi_o$, activation rises from zero at $\psi_d$ to one at $\psi_w$:
\begin{equation}
A(\psi)=\left[\frac{\log|\psi_d/\psi|}{\log|\psi_d/\psi_w|}\right]^a
\quad(\psi_d<\psi<\psi_w),
\end{equation}
with zero and one outside the ramp and shape exponent $a>0$. Thresholds and the ramp require local physiological evaluation.

\subsection{Gas exchange and resource admission}
Millington--Quirk diffusivity is $D_g=D_{0,g}(\phi-\theta)^{10/3}/\phi^2$ \cite{millington1961permeability}. For reference reaction coefficient $k_{g,\mathrm{ref}}$, temperature response $Q_{10}$ and reference temperature $T_{\mathrm{ref}}$, we set $k_g=k_{g,\mathrm{ref}}A(\psi)\allowbreak Q_{10}^{(T-T_{\mathrm{ref}})/10}$. A homogeneous first-order layer with atmospheric concentration $C_{a,g}$ at the surface and zero basal concentration gradient has uptake
\begin{equation}
J_g=C_{a,g}\sqrt{D_gk_g}\tanh\left(L\sqrt{k_g/D_g}\right).
\end{equation}
Hydrogen and CO oxidation consume half a mole of oxygen per mole of donor. A prescribed oxygen limit scales their fluxes proportionally when required. Chemical power is $202000J_{\mathrm{H_2}}+243000J_{\mathrm{CO}}$~W~m$^{-2}$, multiplied by a supplied capture fraction. Reaction coefficients are independent inputs.

The separate resource ceiling maximizes carbohydrate-carbon flux $v_C$ using hydrogen respiration $v_H$ and CO respiration $v_{CO}$. For hydrogen, CO, oxygen and external carbon-dioxide supply bounds $H,C_O,O,C_{\mathrm{ext}}$, capture fraction $\eta$, maintenance demand $M$ and synthesis cost $\epsilon_C$, constraints are
\begin{align}
v_H+2v_C&\leq H,& v_{CO}&\leq C_O,\\
(v_H+v_{CO})/2&\leq O,& v_C-v_{CO}&\leq C_{\mathrm{ext}},\\
\eta(202000v_H+243000v_{CO})&\geq M+\epsilon_Cv_C.
\end{align}
The underlying balanced reactions are hydrogen oxidation, CO oxidation and $2\mathrm{H_2}+\mathrm{CO_2}\rightarrow\mathrm{CH_2O}+\mathrm{H_2O}$. All fluxes are nonnegative. The query requires entailed hydrogenase-plus-CBB support and sets $v_{CO}=0$ without CO-carrier entailment. HiGHS solves the scaled linear programme, with post-solution elemental and constraint residual checks \cite{huangfu2018highs}. The ceiling uses supplied flux bounds and is reported separately from realized process uptake. Ecosystem carbon removal is a separate measured stock-change account requiring imports, geological carbon, counterfactual change and lifecycle emissions.

The schedule comparison crosses $b\in\{3,5\}$, $\psi_d\in\{-70,-100\}$~MPa, $a\in\{0.5,2\}$ and kinetic multipliers $\{0.1,1,10\}$. The schedules use a 14-day horizon, initial water 1.8~mm, temperature $25~^\circ$C and evaporation 1~mm~day$^{-1}$. Three schedules add 12~mm as one pulse, four pulses or daily pulses; the control adds none. Pulses fall on days 0, 3, 7 and 10 for the four-pulse schedule and on day 0 for the single pulse.

\subsection{External datasets, observation maps and validation splits}
We retrieved the primary supplementary data for Buzzard et al. and Tribbia et al. through the PMC public cloud service and verified their publisher-supplied checksums \cite{buzzard2022h2uptake,tribbia2026namib}. The Sonoran analysis uses the authors' corrected response-factor column, with sign reversed so that uptake is positive. Controlled incubation data comprise four source batches within each of four plots at three moisture levels (48 bottles). Hydration contrasts match the source batch, and uncertainty resamples the four source batches within each plot (5,000 draws). The four plots belong to one residence. Gravimetric-to-volumetric conversion uses an explicitly assumed bulk density, with sensitivity values 1.2, 1.5 and 1.8~g~cm$^{-3}$.

Sonoran field data comprise four collars per plot at four campaigns. Because collars within a plot--campaign share the sensor-derived environmental inputs, predictions are evaluated against 16 plot--campaign means. Each test fold holds out an entire campaign. A bounded scalar fit selects the kinetic coefficient on training observations. The soil-response calibration additionally searches porosity $\{0.35,0.40,0.50\}$, retention exponent $\{2,4,6\}$, entry potentials of $-0.0001$, $-0.001$ and $-0.01$~MPa and $Q_{10}\in\{1,2\}$, selecting the lowest training mean squared error. The kinetic search is bounded between $10^{-9}$ and $10^2$~s$^{-1}$. The diffusion-only comparator fits its coefficient on identical training data. A training mean and ordinary least-squares model with intercept, moisture and temperature supply empirical baselines. Errors are descriptive for the four-campaign dataset; campaign holdout includes interpolation across seasons.

Namib supplementary tables provide 50 wet and ten dry atmospheric oxidation rates. Dry rates are quantified for two of the ten sites. The remaining dry endpoints remain unquantified, consistent with the reported failure to reach sub-atmospheric concentrations during the observation period. Wet and dry assays use separate subsamples, so fold-change intervals independently resample five observations per condition within each site. The conversion from the first-order coefficient to mass-normalized uptake uses the reported headspace gas amount, atmospheric hydrogen mixing ratio and 2~g soil mass. Gravimetric soil moisture uses the workbook's percentage cell format. Historical field water potential and climatological soil-moisture estimates are excluded as direct measurements of bottle conditions.

For the temperate comparison, we fit $C(t)=C(0)e^{-kt}$ to each of 77 dilution-corrected bottle time series, retaining negative fitted rates. A nonnegative common amplitude multiplies either the air-pore diffusion ratio or its product with activation at $\psi_d=-85$~MPa. Each fold fits two soils and predicts the third. The threshold was informed by the same study. All external tests preserve native endpoint units, and source files, exclusions, parameter fits and row-level predictions are released. The retrospective evaluation protocol was written after reading the source studies and before numerical fitting.

\subsection{Composition with external models}
An external model is registered as a pure function with typed input and output ports, each given by a name, an ontology term and a unit, together with closed input validity intervals, an optional deductive gate class $C$ and the identifiers of the constants it uses. Port terms are the ChEBI and UO identifiers of the twin's annotation mappings or, for composition-layer quantities, project terms. Wiring requires identical terms and units, and unit conversions are themselves registered models. A composition wires upstream management translations into a twin intervention and passes the resulting trajectory to downstream endpoint models. The composite graph must be acyclic, and any feedback from a downstream output to a twin input within a run is rejected.

An input outside a port's closed interval, or a twin trajectory that violates admissibility or the dilute osmotic domain, returns a structured refusal naming the offending port, value and interval. When a downstream model declares a deductive gate $C$ and the selected sample has $q_s(C)=0$, the chain returns zero with deduced provenance and the ontological justification without evaluating the downstream function. Every output carries one of three provenance labels: twin, external model or deduced.

For atmospheric perturbation chains, the surface concentrations of H$_2$ and CO are promoted from fixed parameters to manipulable exogenous daily forcing inputs of $J_{\mathrm{H_2},t}$ and $J_{\mathrm{CO},t}$. Inoculation is a constitutive intervention on community composition at fixed ontology $K$ and genome evidence $E$. Introducing a catalogued MAG $m^\star\in\mathcal{M}$ at relative share $p$ in sample $s$ defines a counterfactual community whose class shares are recomputed from the existing memberships $D_K(E)$:
\begin{equation}
q'_s(C)=(1-p)q_s(C)+p\mathbf{1}[m^\star\in D_K(E)(C)],
\end{equation}
from which the community factor $\gamma'_g$ is recomputed before the causal mechanisms run.

The external constants are recorded in a registry with a central value, low and high bounds, unit, source and one of three verification statuses: verified in the cited source, modeller assumption, or source not checked. We audited each constant against its cited source. The upstream and downstream models of each chain, their constants and the Hada Al-Sham digitisation are specified in Suppl. Section~\ref{S-sec:composition}. We digitised Fig.~1 of de Z\'elicourt et al. \cite{dezelicourt2018sa187} with a pixel-level digitiser whose reconstruction reproduces the printed percentages to within 0.83 percentage points, and report the digitisation envelope with each yield ratio.

\subsection{Management questions and joint uncertainty}
Each of the 14 questions is a set of composed chains evaluated against a common baseline: a single 12~mm rain event on day 0, initial water storage 1.8~mm, soil temperature 25~$^\circ$C and evaporation demand 1~mm~day$^{-1}$ over 14 days. A question comprises one or more series of scenario levels, such as irrigation intervals of 1, 3 and 10 days, and a headline level and output that the question's answer reports (Table~\ref{tab:answers}; Suppl. Section~\ref{S-sec:uq}).

Each scenario level is evaluated over the union of the constants referenced by its external models, assigned triangular distributions on their low, central and high values, and the 13 twin process parameters, all of which are modeller assumptions (Suppl. Table~\ref{S-tab:v08_parameters}). Parameters clamped by the intervention, such as porosity under biochar and compaction, are excluded. The rate constants $k_{\mathrm{H_2}}$ and $k_{\mathrm{CO}}$ follow log-uniform distributions on $[0.0001,0.01]$~s$^{-1}$ around $0.001$~s$^{-1}$; $\psi_e$ and $\psi_w$ follow log-uniform distributions on magnitude over $[-0.01,-0.0001]$ and $[-0.3,-0.03]$~MPa; $Q_{10}$ is uniform on $[1,2]$; and $b\in[2,6]$, $\psi_d\in[-100,-70]$~MPa, $a\in[0.5,2]$, $D_{\mathrm{H_2}}$, $D_{\mathrm{CO}}$, $d\in[0.3,0.7]$~day$^{-1}$, $f\in[0.4,0.6]$ and $\phi\in[0.35,0.5]$ are triangular around their contract defaults.

A scrambled Sobol' quasi-Monte Carlo Saltelli design with base sample size $N=1,024$ generates $N(d+5)$ runs per level for $d$ active inputs. The design comprises matrices $A$ and $B$, $d$ single-input cross-matrices $A_B^{(i)}$ and three group cross-matrices $A_B^{(G)}$ that resample all inputs of one verification status simultaneously. Intervened and baseline runs share each parameter draw, and contrasts and percentage changes are computed run by run. Medians, 5--95\% intervals and the shares of draws with negative, zero and positive values are computed over the $2N$ draws of $A$ and $B$. First-order ($S_1$) and total-order ($S_T$) Sobol' indices are estimated by the Saltelli and Jansen estimators \cite{saltelli2010variance,jansen1999analysis} with 500 percentile-bootstrap draws (95\% intervals; seed derived from the question, series and level). Outputs that are identical in every draw are reported without indices. A direction is reported as determined when at least 95\% of draws share its sign; a statement that an effect holds in every draw requires a share of exactly one.

Convergence is checked by comparing the 5--95\% interval at $N/2$ and $N$. The 7 non-discrete levels whose interval endpoints shifted by more than 5\% of the full-$N$ interval width were rerun at $N=2,048$ (171 array tasks), leaving 7 of 1,383 outputs flagged (endpoint shifts of 5.5\% to 10.3\%). When fewer than 5\% of draws violate a validity or osmotic domain, quantiles and indices are computed on admitted draws and the refusal fraction is reported; when at least 5\% are refused, $S_1$ and $S_T$ are computed on the binary refusal indicator. Outputs blocked by a closed deductive gate return exact zero with interval $[0,0]$ and deduced provenance, without Monte Carlo variance. The intervals quantify parameter and constant uncertainty at fixed model structure. The full design (1,219 tasks, \UQEvaluations{} evaluations) was executed deterministically on a Slurm cluster. In a 25-task cross-machine check (20,011 scalar outputs), raw floating-point values agreed to within $2.0\times 10^{-11}$ relative difference, and published summaries are rounded to 6 significant digits.
